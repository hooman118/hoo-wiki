## How to Do Great Work 怎么干大事

July 7月 2023 

If you collected lists of techniques for doing great work in a lot of different fields, what would the intersection look like? I decided to find out by making it.

如果你从许多不同领域收集了一些关于如何做大事儿的技巧列表，它们之间会有什么共同点呢？我决定自己动手来找出这个问题的答案。

Partly my goal was to create a guide that could be used by someone working in any field. But I was also curious about the shape of the intersection. And one thing this exercise shows is that it does have a definite shape; it's not just a point labelled "work hard."

部分来说，我的目的是打算创建一个任何领域都通用的指南。但与此同时，我也对这交集到底是个什么样子的东西感到好奇。这次尝试让我知道，它确实有那么一个明确的样子；而不仅仅只是一个写着“努力工作”四个字的小标签而已。

The following recipe assumes you're very ambitious.

没错，以下配方正是为雄心勃勃的你所准备的。

The first step is to decide what to work on. The work you choose needs to have three qualities: it has to be something you have a natural aptitude for, that you have a deep interest in, and that offers scope to do great work.

第一步是决定要干啥。你的选择需要具备三个特点：

* 在这件事儿上，你必须得有那么点天赋
* 你对它非常感兴趣
* 它有机会干着干着变成一件大事儿

In practice you don't have to worry much about the third criterion. Ambitious people are if anything already too conservative about it. So all you need to do is find something you have an aptitude for and great interest in. [^1]

实际上，你一般不太需要担心第三条。野心勃勃的人在这方面，反而可能过于保守。所以你需要做的就是找到一个你有天赋并且非常感兴趣的事情。[^1]

That sounds straightforward, but it's often quite difficult. When you're young you don't know what you're good at or what different kinds of work are like. Some kinds of work you end up doing may not even exist yet. So while some people know what they want to do at 14, most have to figure it out.

这听起来很简单，但实际上通常相当困难。当你年轻的时候，你并不知道自己擅长什么，或者不知道不同类型的工作都是些啥。你最后去做的某些工作那时候甚至可能还压根儿不存在。所以，虽然有些幸运儿14岁就知道自己想做什么，但大部分人都不得不努力地去寻找发掘。

The way to figure out what to work on is by working. If you're not sure what to work on, guess. But pick something and get going. You'll probably guess wrong some of the time, but that's fine. It's good to know about multiple things; some of the biggest discoveries come from noticing connections between different fields.

想找到你要去干什么事儿，最好的方法就是去干。如果你拿不准，那就猜猜看。但一定得选定一件事儿并且开干。有时候你可能会猜错，但没关系。了解各种各样的事情本身就是件好事儿；一些最伟大的发现，通常来自于某人注意到了这不同领域之间的关联之处。

Develop a habit of working on your own projects. Don't let "work" mean something other people tell you to do. If you do manage to do great work one day, it will probably be on a project of your own. It may be within some bigger project, but you'll be driving your part of it.

养成为自己项目工作的习惯。不要让“工作”仅仅意味着他人告诉你要做的事情。如果你真的有一天能够干大事儿，那么它很大概率上就是你自己的项目。它也可能是属于某个更大的项目，但是由你所主导的那一部分。

What should your projects be? Whatever seems to you excitingly ambitious. As you grow older and your taste in projects evolves, exciting and important will converge. At 7 it may seem excitingly ambitious to build huge things out of Lego, then at 14 to teach yourself calculus, till at 21 you're starting to explore unanswered questions in physics. But always preserve excitingness.

那么你自己的项目应该是什么呢？任何对你来说感觉充满挑战的事情。随着年龄的增长、以及品味的提高，令人兴奋与重要性将逐渐合二为一。7岁时，用乐高积木建造个大家伙可能是让你兴奋的事情；然后14岁时，变成了自学微积分；到了21岁，你已经开始探索物理学中的未解之谜了。但是，请永远保持激情在线。

There's a kind of excited curiosity that's both the engine and the rudder of great work. It will not only drive you, but if you let it have its way, will also show you what to work on.

有一种兴奋的好奇心，它既是干大事的引擎，也是方向盘。它不仅会推动你前行，而且如果你放手让它带路，它还会告诉你应该去做什么。

What are you excessively curious about — curious to a degree that would bore most other people? That's what you're looking for.

你对哪些事情有着特别的好奇心——特别到大多数人对此都感到无聊的程度？这就是你要寻找的东西。

Once you've found something you're excessively interested in, the next step is to learn enough about it to get you to one of the frontiers of knowledge. Knowledge expands fractally, and from a distance its edges look smooth, but once you learn enough to get close to one, they turn out to be full of gaps.

一旦你找到了自己特别感兴趣的东西，下一步就是学习足够多的知识，直到你接近知识的最前沿。知识的扩展过程像是一种“分形”，从远处看，那边缘看起来很平滑，但当你学到足够多，凑近了看它时，你会发现它到处都是缺口与空白。

The next step is to notice them. This takes some skill, because your brain wants to ignore such gaps in order to make a simpler model of the world. Many discoveries have come from asking questions about things that everyone else took for granted. [^2]

然后，下一步就是，你得去注意到这些缺口与空白之处。这需要一些技巧，因为你的大脑天生就会想要忽略这些缺口和空白，好形成一个更简单的世界模型。许多发现，都来自于针对那些大家都视为理所当然的东西，提出问题。[^2]

If the answers seem strange, so much the better. Great work often has a tincture of strangeness. You see this from painting to math. It would be affected to try to manufacture it, but if it appears, embrace it.

如果问题的答案看起来很奇怪，那就更好了。牛B大事，往往自带一丝奇怪的气息。从绘画到数学都是如此。刻意制造出奇怪来，可能显得矫情，但如果这是自然而然出现的，抱紧它。

Boldly chase outlier ideas, even if other people aren't interested in them — in fact, especially if they aren't. If you're excited about some possibility that everyone else ignores, and you have enough expertise to say precisely what they're all overlooking, that's as good a bet as you'll find. [^3]

大胆地追求非主流的想法，即使其他人对它们不感兴趣 —— 实际上，尤其是其它人不感兴趣的时候。如果你对某种可能性感到兴奋，而所有其他人都忽略了，并且你有足够的专业知识，能精确地说出他们都忽略了什么，那么这就是你能找到的最好机会。[^3]

Four steps: choose a field, learn enough to get to the frontier, notice gaps, explore promising ones. This is how practically everyone who's done great work has done it, from painters to physicists.

四个步骤：

1. 选择一个领域
2. 学到足够多，直到逼近前沿
3. 注意缺口与空白
4. 探索有前景的部分

从画家到物理学家，几乎所有干成大事儿的人都是这么干的。

Steps two and four will require hard work. It may not be possible to prove that you have to work hard to do great things, but the empirical evidence is on the scale of the evidence for mortality. That's why it's essential to work on something you're deeply interested in. Interest will drive you to work harder than mere diligence ever could.

第二步和第四步需要努力工作。可能我没法证明你必须努力工作才能干大事儿，但从经验上看，这和证明「人终有一死」是一回事儿。这就是为什么我说，你需要做那些自己特别感兴趣的事情。兴趣会让你比单纯的勤奋，干得更加努力。

The three most powerful motives are curiosity, delight, and the desire to do something impressive. Sometimes they converge, and that combination is the most powerful of all.

三个最强大的驱动力分别是好奇心、快乐和想要做点什么去得瑟一下的愿望。有时它们会三位一体，合而为一，成为是世界上最强大的驱动力。

The big prize is to discover a new fractal bud. You notice a crack in the surface of knowledge, pry it open, and there's a whole world inside.

最大的奖励是你发现了一个新的「分形芽」。你注意到当前人类知识边界上的一个裂缝，打开之后你发现，这里面是一个完整的新世界。

Let's talk a little more about the complicated business of figuring out what to work on. The main reason it's hard is that you can't tell what most kinds of work are like except by doing them. Which means the four steps overlap: you may have to work at something for years before you know how much you like it or how good you are at it. And in the meantime you're not doing, and thus not learning about, most other kinds of work. So in the worst case you choose late based on very incomplete information. [^4]

让我们再多聊一下关于「如何决定要干啥？」这个复杂问题。最主要的困难在于，除非你真的去干，否则你无法判断大多数工作是什么样的。这意味着前面说的四个步骤是搅在一块儿的：你可能干一件事儿很多年，然后才知道你是不是喜欢它，或者你是不是擅长它。而与此同时，你没法干（也因此没法了解）大部分其他类型的事情。所以最糟糕的情况是，你基于非常不完整的信息做选择，还选择晚了。[4]

The nature of ambition exacerbates this problem. Ambition comes in two forms, one that precedes interest in the subject and one that grows out of it. Most people who do great work have a mix, and the more you have of the former, the harder it will be to decide what to do.

雄心壮志的本质加剧了这个问题。所谓「雄心壮志」有两种形式，一种是在对主题产生兴趣之前就有的，另一种则是基于兴趣产生的。干大事儿的人都混合着这两种「雄心壮志」，前一种「雄心壮志」越多，你想要选择干啥，就会越困难。

The educational systems in most countries pretend it's easy. They expect you to commit to a field long before you could know what it's really like. And as a result an ambitious person on an optimal trajectory will often read to the system as an instance of breakage.

大多数国家的教育系统都假装这选择很容易。他们期望你在真正了解之前就做出选择和承诺。结果，当一个雄心勃勃的家伙，走在自己的最佳路线上时，往往会被教育系统视为一个问题。

It would be better if they at least admitted it — if they admitted that the system not only can't do much to help you figure out what to work on, but is designed on the assumption that you'll somehow magically guess as a teenager. They don't tell you, but I will: when it comes to figuring out what to work on, you're on your own. Some people get lucky and do guess correctly, but the rest will find themselves scrambling diagonally across tracks laid down on the assumption that everyone does.

如果他们至少能承认这一点，那还能好点——如果他们承认：**教育系统不仅不能帮助你选定应该从事什么工作，而且整个教育系统还是基于「你在十几岁时就能魔术般地猜到自己应该做什么」这个假设而设计的**。他们不会承认的，不过我会告诉你：**当涉及到决定从事什么工作时，你只能靠自己。**有些人很幸运，蒙对了，但其他人则会发现自己躺倒在那些假设人人都会走的轨道上，歪着身子、滑来滑去。

What should you do if you're young and ambitious but don't know what to work on? What you should not do is drift along passively, assuming the problem will solve itself. You need to take action. But there is no systematic procedure you can follow. When you read biographies of people who've done great work, it's remarkable how much luck is involved. They discover what to work on as a result of a chance meeting, or by reading a book they happen to pick up. So you need to make yourself a big target for luck, and the way to do that is to be curious. Try lots of things, meet lots of people, read lots of books, ask lots of questions. [^5]

如果你还年轻、雄心勃勃，但不知道要做什么，你该怎么办呢？你绝不应该被动地任自己在那里打滑，期望问题会自动解决。你需要行动。但是，并不存在一个系统的、可以让你一步一步照着做的流程。当你读那些干成过大事儿的伟人传记时，你会发现，满纸写的都是「运气」两个大字。他们碰巧遇到了某人，或恰巧读到了一本书，于是就发现了自己应该做什么。所以，你需要为自己的「运气」制造一个巨大的目标，方法就是保持好奇心。尝试各种事，结识各种人，读各种书籍，提各种问题。[^5]

When in doubt, optimize for interestingness. Fields change as you learn more about them. What mathematicians do, for example, is very different from what you do in high school math classes. So you need to give different types of work a chance to show you what they're like. But a field should become increasingly interesting as you learn more about it. If it doesn't, it's probably not for you.

当你拿不准时，围着兴趣优化。随着你对一些领域了解得更深入，它也会发生变化。比如，数学家做的事情，和你在高中数学课上所做的完全不同。所以，你需要给不同类型的工作一个机会，让它们露出真实面目。但是，当你对一个领域了解得越多时，它应该变得越来越有趣。如果不是这样，那么它可能不适合你。

Don't worry if you find you're interested in different things than other people. The stranger your tastes in interestingness, the better. Strange tastes are often strong ones, and a strong taste for work means you'll be productive. And you're more likely to find new things if you're looking where few have looked before.

如果你发现自己感兴趣的事情，其他人并不感兴趣，别担心。你的兴趣越奇葩越好。越是奇葩兴趣往往越强烈，而强烈的兴趣意味着你会很有生产力。如果你在少有人探索过的地方寻宝，你更有可能找到新东西。

One sign that you're suited for some kind of work is when you like even the parts that other people find tedious or frightening.

一个表明你适合某种工作的信号是：你甚至会喜欢这工作中别人觉得枯燥，或者恐怖的那一部分。

But fields aren't people; you don't owe them any loyalty. If in the course of working on one thing you discover another that's more exciting, don't be afraid to switch.

但领域不是人；你不欠它们任何忠诚。如果在做某事的过程中，你发现了另一件更令人兴奋的事，不要害怕，换就是了。

If you're making something for people, make sure it's something they actually want. The best way to do this is to make something you yourself want. Write the story you want to read; build the tool you want to use. Since your friends probably have similar interests, this will also get you your initial audience.

如果你在为人们创作点什么东西，要确保这是他们真正想要的。做到这一点的最好方法，是为自己做你自己想要的东西。写你想读的故事；构建你想用的工具。因为你的朋友可能会有类似的兴趣，这也会为你带来你的最初的受众。

This should follow from the excitingness rule. Obviously the most exciting story to write will be the one you want to read. The reason I mention this case explicitly is that so many people get it wrong. Instead of making what they want, they try to make what some imaginary, more sophisticated audience wants. And once you go down that route, you're lost. [^6]

这得遵循“兴奋定律”。显然，最让你兴奋得必须写下来的故事，就是你自己想读的那个。我之所以特地说这事儿，是因为有很多人都理解岔了。他们不是创作他们自己想要的东西，而是试图创作一些想象中的、更成熟的受众想要的东西。一旦你选了那条路，你就迷失了。[^6]

There are a lot of forces that will lead you astray when you're trying to figure out what to work on. Pretentiousness, fashion, fear, money, politics, other people's wishes, eminent frauds. But if you stick to what you find genuinely interesting, you'll be proof against all of them. If you're interested, you're not astray.

当你试图弄清楚要该干什么时，有很多力量会把你带入歧途。矫饰、时尚、恐惧、金钱、政治、他人的愿望、声名显赫的骗子等等。但如果你坚持只做自己真正感兴趣的事情，你就可以对所有这些免疫。如果你依旧兴致盎然，那么你肯定没迷路。

Notes

[^1]: I don't think you could give a precise definition of what counts as great work. Doing great work means doing something important so well that you expand people's ideas of what's possible. But there's no threshold for importance. It's a matter of degree, and often hard to judge at the time anyway. So I'd rather people focused on developing their interests rather than worrying about whether they're important or not. Just try to do something amazing, and leave it to future generations to say if you succeeded. 我不认为你可以给出什么是大事的准确定义。干大事意味着把一些重要的事情干得如此之好，以至于你扩大了人们对可能性的认知。但是，重要性并没有一个固定的阈值。它是一个程度问题，并且很难在当时做出判断。所以，我宁愿人们专注于发展自己的兴趣，而不是担心它们是否重要。只管试着去做一些有意思的事情，是非成败留待后人评说。
[^2]:  A lot of standup comedy is based on noticing anomalies in everyday life. "Did you ever notice...?" New ideas come from doing this about nontrivial things. Which may help explain why people's reaction to a new idea is often the first half of laughing: Ha! 很多的脱口秀段子都来自观察日常生活中的反常现象。"你有没有注意到...?" 新的想法来自于用种的方法去观察那些有深度的事物。这或许可以解释为什么人们对一个新想法的反应往往是笑声的前半部分：哈！
[^3]: That second qualifier is critical. If you're excited about something most authorities discount, but you can't give a more precise explanation than "they don't get it," then you're starting to drift into the territory of cranks. 那第二个限定词非常关键。如果你对大部分权威机构忽视的事物感到兴奋，但你不能给出比“他们不懂”更精确的解释，那么你正在滑入偏执狂的领域。
[^4]: Finding something to work on is not simply a matter of finding a match between the current version of you and a list of known problems. You'll often have to coevolve with the problem. That's why it can sometimes be so hard to figure out what to work on. The search space is huge. It's the cartesian product of all possible types of work, both known and yet to be discovered, and all possible future versions of you. 找到点事儿做并不仅仅是把当前版本的你和一系列已知问题匹配起来。你经常需要与问题共同进化。这就是为什么有时确定要做什么是如此困难。搜索的范围是巨大的。它是所有可能的工作类型（已知的和尚未发现的）与所有可能的你的未来版本的笛卡尔乘积。There's no way you could search this whole space, so you have to rely on heuristics to generate promising paths through it and hope the best matches will be clustered. Which they will not always be; different types of work have been collected together as much by accidents of history as by the intrinsic similarities between them. 你无法搜索整个空间，所以你必须依赖启发式方法来在其中找到有前景的通路，并希望最佳匹配会聚合在一起。但它们并不总是这样；不同类型的工作被汇集在一起，很大程度上是由历史的偶然性，而不是它们之间的内在相似性所决定的。
[^5]: There are many reasons curious people are more likely to do great work, but one of the more subtle is that, by casting a wide net, they're more likely to find the right thing to work on in the first place. 好奇心强的人更有可能干大事儿的原因很多，但其中一个更为微妙的原因是，因为广撒网，他们有更大概率首先找到正确的事儿干。
[^6]: It can also be dangerous to make things for an audience you feel is less sophisticated than you, if that causes you to talk down to them. You can make a lot of money doing that, if you do it in a sufficiently cynical way, but it's not the route to great work. Not that anyone using this m.o. would care. 为那些你认为比自己愚蠢的受众来创作东西也可能是危险的，特别是如果这导致你轻视他们。如果你以一种足够冷酷的方式去这么做，可以赚很多钱，但这不是干大事的通路。当然，这么干事儿的人压根儿不会关心这个。
