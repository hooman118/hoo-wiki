## The Digital Economy 数字经济

Over the past decade, there has been a big shift in how we do work. More opportunities have opened up, remote work is common, and technological advancements have begun to replace labor-intensive jobs with creative work.

在过去的十年里，我们的工作方式发生了巨大变化。更多的机会被打开了，远程工作变得普遍，技术进步也开始用创造性的工作替代劳动密集型的岗位。

There is more opportunity than ever, but only for those that are open to shedding their prior beliefs on what "work" is.

机会比以往任何时候都多，但这些机会只属于那些愿意抛弃对“工作”这一概念的旧有观念的人。

During this shift, you may have picked up beliefs that make you think :

- You need startup capital
- You need a lot of experience
- You need a lot of followers
- You need a physical product or service location
- You need to avoid competition and saturation
- You have to be neurotic about profit margins, operating costs, and supply chains

在这一转变过程中，你可能形成了一些让你认为以下内容的观念：

- 你需要启动资金
- 你需要大量经验
- 你需要大量粉丝
- 你需要一个实物产品或服务地点
- 你需要避免竞争和市场饱和
- 你必须对利润率、运营成本和供应链保持神经质的关注

All of this could not be further from the truth.

这些都离事实相去甚远。

If you are more exposed and open to the digital economy, you may also think:

- You have to be extremely good-looking to build an audience
- You need to be insightful, wise, and produce Jordan Peterson level ideas
- You have to be a skilled writer, speaker, video producer, and have all of this fancy equipment that is reserved to those that have money to spend on it.

如果你对数字经济有更多的接触和了解，你可能还会认为：

- 你必须非常好看才能建立起观众群
- 你需要有深刻的见解、智慧，得像乔丹·彼得森那样提出高水平的观点
- 你必须是一个优秀的作家、演讲者、视频制作人，还得拥有那些只有有钱人才能买得起的高端设备

Again, none of these are true.

同样，这些都不是真相。

All of this really is as simple as you make it, but we all know how hard that can be.

这一切其实可以简单得多，但我们都知道做到这一点有多难。

In the coming sections and modules, I will be helping you shed these limiting beliefs while giving you simple systems to help you thrive in this new economy.

在接下来的章节和模块中，我将帮助你摆脱这些限制性信念，同时提供一些简单的系统，帮助你在这一新经济中蓬勃发展。

> #### Assumptions Are Your Worst Enemy 假设是你最大的敌人
>
> If you have not experienced a common saying or piece of advice — **you have no business adopting that as a hardset belief or assumption.**
>
> 如果你没有亲身体验过某句常见的格言或建议——**你没有理由将其视为坚不可摧的信念或假设。**
>
> Open your mind, test it out, and find out for yourself whether it is true or not.
>
> 打开你的心态，亲自去测试，自己发现它是否真实。

### The Reality Of The Online World We Have Created 我们创造的在线世界的现实

This course is focused on the digital aspect of all of this.

本课程专注于其中的数字层面。

While physical products are great — and can 100% be sold from your brand — it is almost a no brainer to have a digital product and service stack as your foundation.

尽管实物产品很好——并且完全可以通过你的品牌销售——但以数字产品和服务组合作为基础几乎是一个毋庸置疑的选择。

Why?

为什么？

- They cost close to nothing to create.
- They can have 90+% profit margins (on the low end if you are a one-person business).
- They don't have a cost for replication. Once you know they sell, all you have to do is increase the amount of traffic being sent to them.
- For something like a freelance or consulting service — you can decrease the amount of time you spend on client work as you build leverage.

- 它们几乎没有制作成本。
- 它们的利润率可以达到90%以上（对于单人企业来说，这是低估了）。
- 它们没有复制成本。一旦你知道它们能卖出去，唯一要做的就是增加引导流量的数量。
- 对于像自由职业或咨询服务这样的业务——随着你逐渐建立起杠杆，你可以减少花在客户工作上的时间。

This gives you full control over your income.

这让你对收入拥有完全的掌控。

Want to make more? Invest time or money into building free traffic sources.

想赚更多？那就投入时间或金钱，构建免费的流量来源。

Like an on / off button for making money.

就像打开/关闭一个赚钱的按钮一样简单。

But how do you get people to see those products?

但你如何让人们看到这些产品？

Thankfully, you don't need to invest in a physical location. In fact, you don't need to invest anything at all.

幸运的是，你不需要投资一个实体店面。事实上，你根本不需要投入任何东西。

The coders of the world got bored of building websites & landing pages and made that skill accessible to anyone.

世界上的程序员们已经厌倦了创建网站和落地页，他们把这种技能变得对任何人都唾手可得。

What about hosting the product, booking calls, and setting up your "store front?"

那产品托管、预定电话以及搭建“店面”呢？

Free as well. No-code tools have made this a possibility.

也是免费的。无代码工具已经让这一切成为可能。

"No-code tools" is just a fancy way of saying software that does the work for you. Like little salesman that are up and running 24/7 without costing you a dime (until you are growing fast enough to invest).

“无代码工具”不过是个花哨的说法，意思是软件帮你完成工作。就像小推销员，全天候运作，却不花你一分钱（除非你发展得够快，需要进一步投资）。

Things like:

- Gumroad for hosting eBooks, courses, and memberships
- Calendly for creating a call calendar and booking link
- Carrd for building fast landing pages to sell your offers better
- Revue or Convertkit for building an email list
- Website builders for building a personal site
- Social media platforms to build an audience (a traffic source like Facebook ads but at zero cost if you know how to use the platform).

例如：

- Gumroad 用来托管电子书、课程和会员项目
- Calendly 用来创建电话日程表和预约链接
- Carrd 用来快速搭建落地页，更好地销售你的产品
- Revue 或 Convertkit 用来构建邮件列表
- 网站建设器 用来搭建个人网站
- 社交媒体平台 用来构建观众群（类似于 Facebook 广告的流量来源，但如果你会用平台，零成本）。

All of them are free, and those are just a few. Most of them only have a cost once you are at a point where you *should* be making more than enough money to justify it.

所有这些工具都是免费的，而这些只是其中的一部分。大多数工具只有在你应该已经赚了足够多的钱时才会产生费用，从而值得投资。

**But Dan, what about experience?**

**但，Dan，经验怎么办？**

*Don't I have to be super skilled or an expert in a specific field?*

*难道我不需要在某个特定领域非常熟练或成为专家吗？*

Well, yes, but there are ways around that. In fact, its more beneficial to start as a beginner.

嗯，的确需要一些技能，但其实有绕开的办法。事实上，从初学者开始更有优势。

A BIG part of building an audience is educating your audience to get to the point that you are at. That's what they want. They want to become a higher value individual using the methods that got you to that point.

构建观众群的一个重要部分是帮助他们提升，达到你所在的水平。这正是他们想要的。他们想通过你所用的方法，成为更有价值的人。

And who else is better to learn from than someone that is 1-2 steps ahead of you?

而谁更适合教他们呢？当然是比他们领先一两步的人。

Someone that is 10 steps ahead of you won't give you the right advice. They are operating on a different playing field.

那些领先十步的人不会给你合适的建议，因为他们已经处于完全不同的游戏规则下。

But someone that is 1 step ahead of you knows exactly how to navigate whatever roadblock someone is facing.

但比你领先一步的人恰好知道如何应对你面临的所有阻碍。

There are tactics and strategies that will help you grow rapidly as a beginner.

有很多策略和技巧可以帮助你作为初学者快速成长。

Example: teaching about a topic that you love studying.

举个例子：教授你热爱研究的话题。

Let's say you listened to a podcast on marketing.

比如说，你听了一期关于市场营销的播客。

You create high-performing content on that (teaching people about it).

你可以基于此创作高效内容（教授他人相关知识）。

You then tag the podcaster that you learned it from. Why?

接着，你标记出你从中学到内容的播客主。为什么要这样做？

- Citing your sources makes your argument more credible (authority).
- The podcaster may like, comment, or retweet — meaning you may gain followers from their big audience.
- The podcaster will remember you, especially if the content was good, meaning you may work with them in the future.

- 引用你的来源会让你的观点更具可信度（权威性）。
- 播客主可能会点赞、评论或转发——这意味着你可能从他们庞大的观众群中获得粉丝。
- 如果你的内容很棒，播客主会记住你，这意味着你未来可能有机会与他们合作。

I've worked with YouTubers and podcasters that I grew up listening to. It is truly surreal when you understand that everyone online is a normal human like you.

我曾经与一些我从小听到大的 YouTuber 和播客主合作。当你理解了每一个网络上的人都和你一样是普通人时，这种体验简直超现实。

They just put in consistent reps that made them seem like some sort of celebrity.

他们只是通过持续不断的努力，让自己看起来像某种名人。

We will learn more about this zero-experience strategy when we talk about Learning In Public.

我们将在谈论“公开学习”时深入了解更多关于这个“零经验策略”的内容。

> #### From Zero To Infinity 从零到无限
>
> You can start from zero.
>
> 你可以从零开始。
>
> You can scale to infinity.
>
> 你可以扩展到无限。
>
> Do not let any thought that tells you otherwise live in your psyche. Better yet, if you have that thought, write about it online to understand it deeper.
>
> 不要让任何告诉你相反的想法在你的心中扎根。更进一步，如果你有这种想法，写在网上，借此深入理解它。

### The Age Of Information  信息时代

One big limiting belief that many have is that they think information is not inherently valuable.

许多人存在的一个限制性信念是，他们认为信息本身并不具备价值。

This shouldn't have to be said — but all we do as human beings is process information. That is literally it.

这本不该需要说明——但作为人类，我们所做的一切就是处理信息。实际上，就是这样。

We go about life, process the information from our experiences, and it either contributes to a better or worse life.

我们经历生活，处理从经验中获得的信息，这些信息或促使我们生活变得更好，或让生活变得更糟。

That's why we spend more time than we should soaking in educational, inspirational, and entertaining content on social media and TV.

这也是为什么我们花费比预期更多的时间，在社交媒体和电视上吸收教育性的、激励性的、以及娱乐性内容的原因。

- When you are socializing, you are exchanging information with others.
- When you are walking down the road, you are exchanging information with the environment.
- When you are alone, you are exchanging information with yourself.

- 当你社交时，你是在与他人交换信息。
- 当你在路上走时，你是在与环境交换信息。
- 当你独处时，你是在与自己交换信息。

Any and all of this can (and should) be turned into content.

所有这些信息都可以（也应该）转化为内容。

Information is everything. It is the foundation of all communication and connection. This is no different online.

信息即一切。它是所有交流和连接的基础。在线上世界也不例外。

However, information overload is a thing.

然而，信息过载确实存在。

When we go over the APAG persuasive writing framework — you will learn how to produce memorable information that improves others' direct human experience.

当我们深入探讨APAG说服性写作框架时——你将学会如何生产能够改善他人直接人类体验的有记忆性的内容。

The human experience is partially determined by information. *Is there anything more worthy of your time than producing information that improves the very foundation of the human experience?*

人类体验的部分是由信息决定的。*还有什么比创造能改善人类体验根基的信息更值得你花费时间呢？*

> #### Make It A Habit 把它变成一种习惯
>
> Be intentional and conscious of all information you are exposed to. Think critically about how that information can be applied to everyday life.
>
> 对你所接触的所有信息有意识且有意图。批判性地思考这些信息如何应用于日常生活。
>
> An entire brand can be built from this alone.
>
> 仅凭这一点，你就可以建立一个完整的品牌。

### The Shift From Corporation To Community 从公司到社区的转变

People are losing trust in the "system."

人们正在逐渐失去对“系统”的信任。

- Formal education is overpriced.
- People are craving human connection and intimacy.
- Everyone is getting tired of centralization in every industry.

- 正规教育费用过高。
- 人们渴望人与人之间的连接与亲密感。
- 每个行业的集中化让人疲惫不堪。

This isn't difficult to notice.

这些现象并不难察觉。

If you follow me on socials and other similar accounts, you should be well aware of this.

如果你关注我以及类似的社交账号，你应该对此非常了解。

You can learn more practical information by watching 20 hours of design-related YouTube videos than with a 4 year design degree.

看20小时的设计相关YouTube视频，你能学到的实用信息比4年的设计学位还多。

This goes for any discipline, not just design or industries that you have been exposed to.

这适用于任何学科，不仅仅是你接触过的设计或其他行业。

Small "boutique" brands with a ~3 person team are pulling in multiple 7-figures per year.

一个大约3人组成的小型“精品”品牌每年能赚取数百万美元。

Personal brands that know what they are doing can pull in $20K per month with 4 hours of work per week.

懂得经营的个人品牌每周工作4小时，每月就能赚2万美元。

As you will learn, your personal brand is what sets you apart. 

正如你将要学到的，你的个人品牌是让你脱颖而出的关键。

Now think about it.

现在想一想。

Do you really want to have a ton of employees and a billion-dollar company?

你真的想要拥有大量员工和一个市值十亿的公司吗？

Or do you want to do what you love and eventually make one million a year (or however much you want to live the life that YOU want).

还是你想做自己热爱的事，并最终每年赚取一百万美元（或者任何你认为能让你过上自己想要的生活的收入）？

To me, it's a no-brainer.

对我来说，这不需要多想。

My job is to educate, entertain, and inspire my following.

我的工作是教育、娱乐和激励我的追随者。

However, I am not creating followers, I am creating new leaders that can evolve as I do.

然而，我不是在创造追随者，我是在培养能够与我一起进化的新领导者。
