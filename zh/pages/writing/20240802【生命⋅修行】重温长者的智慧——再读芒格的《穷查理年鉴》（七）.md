## 其他要点总结：

### 经济学的优点

**1. 跨学科的整合能力**：
经济学是软科学中的佼佼者，吸引了优秀的人才，并且与现实世界的实践紧密结合。芒格特别赞赏经济学能够从其他学科借鉴概念，如加勒特·哈丁的“公地悲剧”模型。

**2. 杰出的语言表达能力**：
经济学历史上涌现出一批优秀的作家，如亚当·斯密和约翰·梅纳德·凯恩斯，他们的作品不仅在经济学领域内产生了深远的影响，而且在更广泛的文化中也得到了认可。

**3. 实际应用与政策影响**：
经济学家在实际政策中发挥了重要作用，如乔治·舒尔茨在内阁中的三次任命，显示了经济学的实用价值。

### 经济学的缺点

**1. 致命的学科脱节和“拿锤子综合症”**：
芒格批评经济学缺乏与其他学科的联系，导致学科间的孤立和片面思维。这种现象导致经济学家过于依赖定量分析，而忽视了定性因素。

**2. 忽视道德和伦理效应**：
芒格指出，经济学常常忽视了美德和恶行在经济系统中的作用。他强调，像复式记账这样的美德效应对经济的改善是显著的，而恶行，如不公平的薪酬制度，则会对经济系统产生破坏性影响。

**3. “物理学嫉妒”**：
芒格批评经济学家试图模仿物理学的精确性，但经济学的复杂性使得这种精确性难以实现。他主张经济学应该接受不确定性，并避免过度简化问题。

**4. 缺乏多学科视角的整合**：
芒格认为，经济学在整合来自不同学科的知识时做得不够。他提到，即便是高级商学院的学生在面对整合性问题时，表现也不尽如人意。

**5. 忽视高阶效应**：
芒格批评经济学家常常忽视二阶及更高阶的效应。他指出，简单的成本预测往往低估了系统中行为变化和激励的影响，导致严重的误判。

**6. 心理学无知**：
经济学在理解和应用心理学原理方面存在严重不足。芒格用心理学在拉美国家重建经济的例子来强调心理学的重要性。—— 可惜我没有找到心理史学中，关于拉美国家的具体例子。

**7. 关注于微观经济学的不足**：
芒格主张经济学需要更多地关注微观经济学，因为它能更好地解释宏观经济学问题，并通过简单的问题演示微观经济学的强大之处。

**8. 对代数基本原则的忽视**：
芒格引入了“伪腐败”的概念，即未被察觉的系统性浪费，认为经济学未能充分应用代数原则去识别和解决类似腐败的问题。

**9. 对美德与恶行效应关注不足**

查理·芒格批评经济学忽视美德和恶行的影响，强调美德（如宗教道德）能增强经济效能，而恶行（如欺诈和泡沫）则损害经济表现。他还提到了制度设计以及不完美的公平性问题。

### 以下是第九次演讲的中英文对照翻译，供参考

#### Talk Nine

**Academic Economics: Strengths and Faults after Considering Interdisciplinary Needs**
**Flerb Kay Undergraduate Lecture University of California, Santa Barbara Economics Department, October 3, 2003**

**查理·芒格 第九次演讲**
**学术经济学：考虑跨学科需求后的优势和缺点**
**弗勒布·凯本科生讲座 加州大学圣塔芭芭拉分校经济学系 2003年10月3日**

---

I have outlined some remarks in a rough way, and after I'm finished talking from that outline, I'll take questions as long as anybody can endure listening, until they drag me away to wherever else I'm supposed to go. As you might guess. I agreed to do this because the subject of getting the soft sciences so they talk better to each other has been one that has interested me for decades. And, of course, economics is, in many respects, the queen of the soft sciences. It's expected to be better than the rest. It's my view that economics is better at the multidisciplinary stuff than the rest of the soft science. And it's also my view that it's still lousy and I'd like to discuss this failure in this talk.

我已经大致列出了我的一些评论，在我按这个提纲讲完之后，我会回答问题，只要有人愿意听，直到他们把我拖到我该去的地方。正如你们所猜测的那样，我同意做这个演讲是因为让软科学更好地相互交流一直是我几十年来感兴趣的课题。当然，在很多方面，经济学是软科学的皇后，应该比其他软科学做得更好。我的看法是，经济学在跨学科方面比其他软科学做得更好。但我也认为它仍然很差，我想在这次演讲中讨论这一失败。

As I talk about strengths and weaknesses in academic economics, one interesting fact you are entitled to know is that I never took a course in economics. And with this striking lack of credentials, you may wonder why I have the chutzpah to be up here giving this talk. The answer is I have a black belt in chutzpah. I was born with it. Some people, like some of the women I know, have a black belt in spending. They were born with that. But what they gave me was a black belt in chutzpah.

当我谈论学术经济学的优缺点时，有一个有趣的事实你们应该知道，那就是我从未上过一门经济学课程。凭借这一显著的缺乏资格，你们可能会想知道我为什么有胆量站在这里做这个演讲。答案是我有“厚颜无耻”的黑带，我天生就有。有些人，比如我认识的一些女性，有“消费”的黑带，她们天生就有。而他们给我的则是“厚颜无耻”的黑带。

But, I come from two peculiar strands of experience that may have given me some useful economic insights. One is Berkshire Hathaway, and the other is my personal educational history. Berkshire, of course, has finally gotten interesting. When Warren took over Berkshire, the market capitalization was about \$10 million. And forty-something years later, there are not many more shares outstanding now than there were then, and the market capitalization is about a \$100 billion, ten thousand for one. And since that has happened, year after year, in kind of a grind-ahead fashion with very few failures, it eventually drew some attention, indicating that maybe Warren and I knew something useful in microeconomics.

但是，我来自两个特殊的经验领域，这可能给了我一些有用的经济见解。一个是伯克希尔·哈撒韦公司，另一个是我的个人教育经历。当然，伯克希尔最终变得有趣了。当沃伦接管伯克希尔时，市值约为1000万美元。而四十多年后，现在流通的股票数量并不比那时多多少，市值约为1000亿美元，增长了一万倍。由于这种情况年复一年地发生，几乎没有失败，最终引起了一些关注，表明也许沃伦和我在微观经济学方面知道一些有用的东西。

For a long time there was a Nobel Prize-winning economist who explained Berkshire Hathaway's success as follows:

很长一段时间里，有一位诺贝尔奖得主的经济学家这样解释伯克希尔哈撒韦的成功：

First, he said Berkshire beat the market in common stock investing through one sigma of luck because nobody could beat the market except by luck. This hard-form version of efficient market theory was taught in most schools of economics at the time. People were taught that nobody could beat the market. Next, the professor went to two sigmas, and three sigmas, and four sigmas, and when he finally got to six sigmas of luck, people were laughing so hard he stopped doing it.
Then, he reversed the explanation 180 degrees. He said, "No, it was still six sigmas, but it was six sigmas of skill." Well, this very sad history demonstrates the truth of Benjamin Franklin's observation in Poor Richard's Almanack, "If you would persuade, appeal to interest and not to reason."

首先，他说伯克希尔在普通股票投资中战胜市场是一种幸运，因为没有人能在市场上取胜，除非是靠运气。这种硬性形式的有效市场理论当时在大多数经济学学校中被教授。人们被教导说没有人能战胜市场。接下来，这位教授提到了两西格玛、三西格玛、四西格玛，当他最终谈到六西格玛的运气时，人们笑得太厉害了，他不得不停下来。然后，他将解释180度大转弯。他说，“不，这仍然是六西格玛，但这是六西格玛的技巧。”这种非常悲哀的历史证明了本杰明·富兰克林在《穷理查德年鉴》中的观察是正确的：“如果你想说服别人，要诉诸利益，而不是诉诸理性。”

The man changed his silly view when his incentives made him change it and not before.

这个人在他的激励因素使他改变之前，改变了他愚蠢的观点。

I watched the same thing happen at the Jules Stein Eye Institute at ULAC. I asked, at one point, "Why are you treating cataracts only with a totally obsolete cataract operation?" And the man said to me, "Charlie, it's such a wonderful operation to teach." When he stopped using that operation, it was because almost all the patients had voted with their feet. Again, appeal to interest and not to reason if you want to change conclusions.

我在加州大学洛杉矶分校的朱尔斯·斯坦眼科研究所也看到了同样的事情。我曾问过，“你们为什么只用完全过时的白内障手术治疗白内障？”那个人对我说，“查理，这是一种非常好的教学手术。”当他停止使用那种手术时，是因为几乎所有的病人都用脚投票。再一次，如果你想改变结论，要诉诸利益，而不是诉诸理性。

Well, Berkshire's whole record has been achieved without paying one ounce of attention to the efficient market theory in its hard form. And not one ounce of attention to the descendants of that idea, which came out of academic economics and went into corporate finance and morphed into such obscenities as the capital asset pricing model, which we also paid no attention to. I think you'd have to believe in the tooth fairy to believe that you could easily outperform the market by seven percentage points per annum just by investing in high-volatility stocks.

好吧，伯克希尔的整个记录是在完全不关注硬性形式的有效市场理论的情况下实现的。我们对这种想法的后代，即从学术经济学中出来并进入公司金融，变成诸如资本资产定价模型等淫秽内容，也没有一丝关注。我认为，如果你认为仅仅通过投资于高波动性股票就可以轻松地每年跑赢市场七个百分点，那你一定相信牙仙的存在。

Yet, believe it or not, like the Jules Stein doctor, people once believed this stuff. And the belief was rewarded. And it spread. And many people still believe it. But Berkshire never paid any attention to it. And now, I think the world is coming our way, and the idea of perfection in all market outcomes is going the way of the dodo.

然而，信不信由你，就像朱尔斯·斯坦医生一样，人们曾经相信这些东西。而且这种信念得到了回报。它传播开来，许多人仍然相信它。但是伯克希尔从未关注过它。现在，我认为世界正在向我们靠拢，而市场结果完美的理念正在走向灭绝。

It was always clear to me that the stock market couldn't be perfectly efficient, because, as a teenager, I'd been to the racetrack in Omaha where they had the parimutuel system. And it was quite obvious to me that if the house take, the croupier's take, was seventeen percent, some people consistently lost a lot less than seventeen percent of all their bets, and other people consistently lost more than seventeen percent of all their bets. So the pari-mutuel system in Omaha had no perfect efficiency. And so I didn't accept the argument that the stock market was always perfectly efficient in creating rational prices.

我一直清楚股市不可能完全有效，因为我少年时在奥马哈的赛马场上见过他们的赔率系统。很明显，如果赌场抽成是百分之十七，有些人始终少输百分之十七的赌注，而其他人始终多输百分之十七的赌注。所以奥马哈的赔率系统没有完全的效率。因此，我不接受股市总是能产生理性价格的观点。

Indeed, there have been some documented cases since of people getting so good at understanding horses and odds that they actually are able to beat the house in off track betting. There aren't many people who can do that, but there are a few people in America who can.

事实上，自那以后，有些人确实能通过理解马匹和赔率来战胜赛马场。能做到这一点的人不多，但在美国有少数人可以做到。

Next, my personal education history is interesting because its deficiencies and my peculiarities eventually created advantages. For some odd reason, I had an early and extreme multidisciplinary cast of mind. I couldn't stand reaching for a small idea in my own discipline when there was a big idea right over the fence in somebody else's discipline. So I just grabbed in all directions for the big ideas that would really work. Nobody taught me to do that; I was just born with that yen. I also was born with a huge craving for synthesis. And when it didn't come easily, which was often, I would rag the problem, and then when I failed, I would put it aside, and I'd come back to it and rag it again. It took me twenty years to figure out how and why cult conversion methods worked. But the psychology departments haven't figured it out yet, so I'm ahead of them.

接下来，我个人的教育历史很有趣，因为它的缺陷和我的独特性最终带来了优势。出于某种奇怪的原因，我很早就有一种极端的跨学科思维方式。我不能忍受在自己的学科中寻找一个小想法，而在其他学科中就有一个大想法。所以我就从各个方向抓住那些真正有用的大想法。没有人教我这样做，我天生就有这种欲望。我也天生渴望综合。当它不容易实现时，我会反复思考这个问题，然后当我失败时，我会把它放在一边，然后再回来反复思考。花了我二十年时间才弄清楚宗教转变方法的运作原理。但心理学系还没弄明白，所以我领先于他们。

But anyway, I have this tendency to want to rag the problems. Because WWII caught me, I drifted into some physics, and the Air Corps sent me to Caltech, where I did a little more physics as part of being made into a meteorologist. And there, at a very young age, I absorbed what I call the fundamental full attribution ethos of hard science. And that was enormously useful to me. Let me explain that ethos.

但无论如何，我有这种倾向，想要解决问题。因为二战，我学了一些物理，空军送我去了加州理工学院，在那里我学了一些物理，作为成为气象学家的一部分。在那个很小的年龄，我吸收了我称之为硬科学的基本完全归因伦理。这对我来说非常有用。让我解释一下这种伦理。

Under this ethos, you've got to know all the big ideas in all the disciplines more fundamental than your own. You can never make any explanation that can be made in a more fundamental way in any other way than the most fundamental way. And you always take with full attribution to the most fundamental ideas that you are required to use. When you're using physics, you say you're using physics. When you're using biology, you say you're using biology. And so on and so on. I could early see that that ethos would act as a fine organizing system for my thought. And I strongly suspected that it would work really well in the soft sciences as well as the hard sciences, so I just grabbed it and used it all through my life in soft science as well as hard science. That was a very lucky idea for me.

根据这种伦理，你必须了解所有比你自己的学科更基本的学科中的所有大想法。你永远不能以比最基本的方法更不基本的方式进行解释。而且你总是完全归因于你需要使用的最基本的想法。当你使用物理学时，你说你在使用物理学。当你使用生物学时，你说你在使用生物学。如此等等。我很早就看到这种伦理可以成为我思维的良好组织系统。我强烈怀疑它在软科学中也能像在硬科学中一样奏效，所以我在软科学和硬科学中都使用了它。这对我来说是一个非常幸运的想法。

Let me explain how extreme that ethos is in hard science. There is a constant, one of the fundamental constants in physics, known as Boltzmann's constant. You probably all know it very well. And the interesting thing about Boltzmann's constant is that Boltzmann didn't discover it. So why is Boltzmann's constant now named for Boltzmann? Well, the answer is that Boltzmann derived that constant from basic physics in a more fundamental way than the poor forgotten fellow who found the constant in the first place in some less fundamental way. The ethos of hard science is so strong in favor of reductionism to the more fundamental body of knowledge that you can wash the discoverer right out of history when somebody else handles his discovery in a more fundamental way. I think that is correct. I think Boltzmann's constant should be named for Boltzmann.

让我解释一下这种伦理在硬科学中的极端性。物理学中有一个常数，被称为玻尔兹曼常数。你们可能都非常了解它。有趣的是，玻尔兹曼并没有发现它。那么，为什么现在这个常数被命名为玻尔兹曼常数呢？答案是，玻尔兹曼从基本物理学中以比最初发现这个常数的可怜的遗忘者更基本的方式推导出了这个常数。硬科学的伦理如此强烈地偏向于简化到更基本的知识体系，以至于当别人以更基本的方式处理他的发现时，你可以将发现者完全从历史中抹去。我认为这是正确的。我认为玻尔兹曼常数应该命名为玻尔兹曼。

[^Boltzmann constant 玻尔兹曼常数 ]: Boltzmann constant derives its name from Austrian physicist Ludwig Boltzmann (1844-1906); it defines the relation between absolute temperature and the kinetic energy contained in each molecule of an ideal gas. In general, the energy in a gas molecule is directly proportional to the absolute temperature. As the temperature increases, the kinetic energy per molecule increases. As a gas is heated, its molecules move more rapidly. This movement produces increased pressure if the gas is confined in a space of constant volume, or increased volume if the pressure remains constant. 玻尔兹曼常数得名于奥地利物理学家路德维希·玻尔兹曼（1844-1906）；它定义了绝对温度与理想气体中每个分子所含动能之间的关系。一般来说，气体分子的能量与绝对温度成正比。随着温度的升高，每个分子的动能增加。当气体被加热时，其分子运动更快。如果气体被限制在恒定体积的空间内，这种运动会产生更高的压力；如果压力保持恒定，则会增加体积。


At any rate, in my history and Berkshire's history, Berkshire went on and on into considerable economic success while ignoring the hard-form efficient markets doctrine once very popular in academic economics and ignoring the descendants of that doctrine in corporate finance where the results became even sillier than they were in economics. This naturally encouraged me.

无论如何，在我的历史和伯克希尔的历史中，伯克希尔在忽视曾经在学术经济学中非常流行的严格形式的有效市场理论的情况下，取得了相当大的经济成功，并忽视了这一理论在公司金融中产生的后代，在那里结果变得比经济学中更加可笑。这自然鼓舞了我。

Finally, with my peculiar history, I'm also bold enough to be here today because, at least when I was young, I wasn't a total klutz. For one year at the Harvard Law School, I was ranked second in a very large group, and I always figured that, while there were always a lot of people much smarter than I was, I didn't have to hang back totally in the thinking game.

最后，鉴于我的特殊历史，我今天站在这里也是因为，至少在我年轻的时候，我不是个完全的笨蛋。在哈佛法学院的一年里，我在一个非常大的群体中排名第二，我一直认为，虽然总有很多人比我聪明得多，但在思考游戏中我不必完全退缩。

Let me begin by discussing the obvious strengths of academic economics. The first obvious strength, and this is true of a lot of places that get repute, is that it was in the right place at the right time. Two hundred years ago, aided by the growth of technology and the growth of other developments in the civilization, the real output per capita of the civilized world started going up at about two percent per annum, compounded. And before that, for the previous thousands of years, it had gone up at a rate that hovered just a hair's breadth above zero. And, of course, economics grew up amid this huge success. Partly it helped the success, and partly it explained it. So, naturally, academic economics grew. And, lately, with the collapse of all the communist economies, as the free market economies or partially free market economies flourished. that added to the reputation of economics. Economics has been a very favorable place to be if you're in academia.

让我先谈谈学术经济学的明显优势。第一个明显的优势，这是很多名声好的地方都有的优势，就是它在正确的时间出现在正确的地方。两百年前，随着技术的发展和文明中其他发展的增长，文明世界的人均实际产出开始以大约每年2%的速度复合增长。在此之前的几千年里，其增长率几乎为零。当然，经济学在这一巨大成功中成长起来。部分地它促进了成功，部分地它解释了成功。因此，学术经济学自然发展起来了。最近，随着所有共产主义经济体的崩溃，自由市场经济或部分自由市场经济的繁荣，这增加了经济学的声誉。如果你在学术界，经济学一直是一个非常有利的地方。

Economics was always more multidisciplinary than the rest of soft science. It just reached out and grabbed things as it needed to. And that tendency to just grab whatever you need from the rest of knowledge if you're an economist has reached a fairly high point in N. Gregory Mankiw's new textbook. I checked out chat textbook. I must have been one of the few businessmen in America that bought it immediately when it came out because it had gotten such a big advance. I wanted to figure out what the guy was doing where he could get an advance that great. So this is how I happened to riffle through Mankiw's freshman textbook. And there I found laid out as principles of economics: Opportunity cost is a superpower, to be used by all people who have any hope of getting the right answer. Also, incentives are superpowers.

经济学一直比其他软科学更具多学科性。它只是根据需要吸收其他领域的知识。而且这种倾向在N. Gregory Mankiw的新教科书中达到了相当高的水平。我查看了那本教科书。我可能是美国为数不多的立即购买它的商人之一，因为它得到了如此大的预付款。我想弄清楚这个人是怎么做到的，他能得到这么大的预付款。所以这就是我翻阅曼昆大一教科书的原因。在那里，我发现经济学的原则是：机会成本是一种超级力量，所有有希望得到正确答案的人都应该使用它。另外，激励也是超级力量。

And lastly the tragedy of the commons model, popularized by longtime friend, UCSB's Garrett Hardin. Hardin caused the delightful introduction into economics-alongside Smith's beneficent invisible hand of Hardin's wicked evildoing invisible foot. Well, I thought that the Hardin model made economics more complete, and I knew when Hardin introduced me to his model, the tragedy of the commons, that it would be in the economics textbooks eventually. And, lo and behold, it finally made it about twenty years later. And it's right for Mankiw to reach out into other disciplines and grab Hardin's model and anything else that works well.

最后是“公地悲剧”模型，由我多年的朋友，加州大学圣塔芭芭拉分校的加勒特·哈丁推广。哈丁将其与亚当·斯密的“看不见的手”并列引入经济学，令人愉悦的是，这种模型最终补充了经济学。我当时就知道，当哈丁向我介绍他的“公地悲剧”模型时，它终将被收入经济学教材中。果然，大约二十年后，它确实成为了教材的一部分。曼昆从其他学科中借用哈丁的模型以及其他有效的模型是正确的。

Another thing that helped economics is that, from the beginning, it attracted the best brains in soft science. Its denizens also interacted more with the practical world than was at all common in soft science and the rest of academia, and that resulted in very creditable outcomes like the three cabinet appointments of economics Ph.D. George Shultz and the cabinet appointment of Larry Summers. So this has been a very favored part of academia.

另一个对经济学有帮助的因素是，经济学从一开始就吸引了软科学中最聪明的人才。经济学领域的人比其他软科学和学术界的其他部分更多地与实际世界互动，导致了许多令人称道的结果，比如经济学博士乔治·舒尔茨三次被任命为内阁成员，以及拉里·萨默斯被任命为内阁成员。因此，这一直是学术界非常受欢迎的领域。

Also, economics early on attracted some of the best writers of language in the history of the Earth. You start out with Adam Smith. Adam Smith was so good a thinker and so good a writer that, in his own time, Emmanuel Kant, then the greatest intellectual in Germany, simply announced that there was nobody in Germany to equal Adam Smith. Well, Voltaire, being an even pithier speaker than Kant, which wouldn't be that hard, immediately said, "Oh well, France doesn't have anybody who can even be compared to Adam Smith." So economics started with some very great men and great writers.

此外，经济学早期就吸引了地球语言历史上一些最优秀的作家。以亚当·斯密为例。他不仅是一位优秀的思想家，也是杰出的作家，以至于在他的时代，德国最伟大的知识分子伊曼纽尔·康德公开宣称德国无人能与亚当·斯密相比。伏尔泰，作为比康德更简洁有力的演说家，立刻表示：“哦，法国也没有人能与亚当·斯密相提并论。”因此，经济学开始就伴随着一些伟大的人物和作家。

And then, there have been later great writers like John Maynard Keynes, whom I quote all the time and who has added a great amount of illumination to my life. And finally, even in the present era, if you take Paul Krugman and read his essays, you will be impressed by his fluency. I can't stand his politics; I'm on the other side. But I love this man's essays. I think Paul Krugman is one of the best essayists alive. And so, economics has constantly attracted these fabulous writers. And they are so good that they have this enormous influence far outside their economic discipline, and that's very uncommon in other academic departments.

后来，还有像约翰·梅纳德·凯恩斯这样伟大的作家，我经常引用他，他为我的生活增添了许多启示。即使在当代，如果你读到保罗·克鲁格曼的文章，你也会对他的流利文笔印象深刻。我无法忍受他的政治立场，因为我处于相对立的一方。但我喜欢他的文章。我认为保罗·克鲁格曼是当今最好的散文家之一。因此，经济学不断吸引这些杰出的作家。他们的才华使他们在经济学之外也产生了巨大影响，而这种现象在其他学术领域中非常少见。

Okay, now it's time to extend criticism instead of praise. We've recognized that economics is better than other soft science academic departments in many ways. And one of the glories of civilization. Now it's only fair that we outline a few things that are wrong with academic economics.

好了，现在该开始批评而不是赞美了。我们已经承认，经济学在许多方面比其他软科学的学术部门要好，这是文明的荣耀之一。现在我们也该公正地指出学术经济学的一些问题了。

What's Wrong with Economics?

经济学的问题是什么？

1) Fatal Unconnectedness, Leading to Man-with-a-Hammer Syndrome, Often Causing Overweighing What Can Be Counted 致命的脱节，导致“拿锤子的人综合症”，经常导致过分强调可计数的事物。

I think I've got eight, no nine objections, some being logical subdivisions of a big general objection. The big general objection to economics was the one early described by Alfred North Whitehead when he spoke of the fatal unconnectedness of academic disciplines, wherein each professor didn't even know the models of the other disciplines, much less try to synthesize those disciplines with his own.

我认为我有八个，不，九个反对意见，有些是大的一般性反对意见的逻辑细分。对经济学的普遍反对意见是阿尔弗雷德·诺斯·怀特海德早期描述的，即学术学科之间的致命脱节，其中每位教授甚至不知道其他学科的模型，更不用说尝试将这些学科与自己的学科综合起来。

I think there's a modern name for this approach that Whitehead didn't like, and that name is "bonkers." This is a perfectly crazy way to behave. Yet economics, like much else in academia. is too insular.

我认为怀特海德不喜欢这种方法的现代名字是“疯狂的”。这是一种完全疯狂的行为方式。然而，经济学和学术界的许多其他领域一样，太过封闭。

The nature of this failure is that it creates what I always call man-with-a-hammer syndrome. And that's taken from the folk saying: To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks pretty much like a nail. And that works marvelously to gum up all professions and all departments of academia and, indeed, most practical life. The only antidote for being an absolute klutz due to the presence of a man-with-a-hammer syndrome is to have a full kit of tools. You don't have just a hammer.

这种失败的性质创造了我常说的“拿着锤子的人综合症”。这来源于民间谚语：对于只有锤子的人来说，每个问题看起来都像钉子。这在所有专业和学术部门以及大多数实际生活中都能有效地制造麻烦。避免因“拿着锤子的人综合症”而成为绝对笨拙的唯一解药是拥有一整套工具，而不仅仅是一把锤子。

[^Alfred North Whitehead 阿尔弗雷德·诺斯·怀特海德 (1861-1947)]: Alfred North Whitehead, a British philosopher and mathematician, worked in logic, mathematics, philosophy of science, and metaphysics. Whitehead is known for developing process philosophy, a view holding that fundamental elements of the universe are occasions of experience. In this view, concrete objects are actually successions of these occasions of experience. By grouping occasions of experience, something as complex as a human being can be defined. Whitehead's views evolved into process theology, a way of understanding God. His best-known mathematics work is Principia Mathematica co-written with Bertrand Russell. 阿尔弗雷德·诺斯·怀特海德是一位英国哲学家和数学家，致力于逻辑、数学、科学哲学和形而上学的研究。怀特海德以发展过程哲学而闻名，这种观点认为宇宙的基本元素是体验的场合。在这种观点中，具体对象实际上是这些体验场合的连续性。通过将体验场合组合起来，可以定义像人类这样复杂的事物。怀特海德的观点演变成过程神学，一种理解上帝的方式。他最著名的数学作品是与伯特兰·罗素合著的《数学原理》。

You've got all the tools. And you've got to have one more trick. You've got to use those tools checklist-style because you'll miss a lot if you just hope that the right tool is going to pop up unaided whenever you need it. But if you've got a full list of tools and go through them in your mind, checklist-style, you will find a lot of answers that you won't find any other way. So limiting this big general objection that so disturbed Alfred North Whitehead is very important, and there are mental tricks that help do the job.

你已经拥有了所有的工具。但你还需要一个诀窍。你必须以清单的方式使用这些工具，因为如果你只是希望在需要时正确的工具能自然而然地出现，你会错过很多东西。但如果你有一份完整的工具清单，并在脑海中以清单的方式逐一检查，你会发现很多答案，这些答案是你无法通过其他方式找到的。所以，限制阿尔弗雷德·诺斯·怀特海德所担心的那个大问题是非常重要的，有一些思维技巧可以帮助完成这项工作。

I think there's a modern name for this approach that Whitehead didn't like, and that name is "bonkers."

我认为怀特海德不喜欢这种方法的现代名字是“疯狂的”。

A special version of this man-with-a-hammer syndrome is terrible, not only in economics but practically everywhere else, including business. It's really terrible in business. You've got a complex system, and it spews out a lot of wonderful numbers that enable you to measure some factors. But there are other factors that are terribly important, [yet] there's no precise numbering you can put to these factors. You know they're important, but you don't have the numbers. well, practically everybody (1) overweighs the stuff that can be numbered because it yields to the statistical techniques they're taught in academia and (2) doesn't mix in the hard-to-measure stuff that may be more important. That is a mistake I've tried all my life to avoid, and I have no regrets for having done that.

这种“拿着锤子的人综合症”的一种特殊版本不仅在经济学中是可怕的，而且几乎在其他任何地方，包括商业中也是如此。在商业中尤其可怕。你有一个复杂的系统，它会生成很多漂亮的数字，使你能够衡量一些因素。但还有其他一些非常重要的因素，你无法为这些因素精确地编号。你知道它们很重要，但你没有数字。实际上，几乎所有人（1）都过度依赖可以量化的东西，因为它们可以使用他们在学术界学到的统计技术，（2）没有混合那些可能更重要但难以衡量的东西。这是我一生中试图避免的错误，我不后悔这样做。

The late, great Thomas Hunt Morgan, who was one of greatest biologists who ever lived, when he got to Caltech, had a very interesting, extreme way of avoiding some mistakes from overcounting what could be measured and undercounting what couldn't. At that time. there were no computers, and the computer substitute then available to science and engineering was the Friden calculator, and Caltech was full of Friden calculators. And Thomas Hunt Morgan banned the Friden calculator from the biology department. And when they said, "What the hell are you doing, Dr. Morgan?" he said, "Well, I am like a guy Who is prospecting for gold along the banks of the Sacramento River in 1849. With a little intelligence, I can reach down and pick up big nuggets of gold. And as long as I can do that, I'm not going to let any people in my department waste scarce resources in placer mining." And that's the way Thomas Hunt Morgan got through life.

已故的伟大生物学家托马斯·亨特·摩根是有史以来最伟大的生物学家之一，当他来到加州理工学院时，他采取了一种非常有趣的极端方式来避免由于过度计算可以测量的东西而低估了不能测量的东西。当时还没有计算机，科学和工程领域可用的计算机替代品是Friden计算器，加州理工学院里到处都是Friden计算器。而托马斯·亨特·摩根禁止在生物系使用Friden计算器。当他们问他：“摩根博士，你到底在干什么？”他回答道：“嗯，我就像1849年在萨克拉门托河岸边淘金的人。凭一点智慧，我可以伸手捡起大块的金块。只要我能做到这一点，我就不会让我的部门里的人浪费稀缺资源进行淘金。”这就是托马斯·亨特·摩根度过一生的方式。

I've adopted the same technique, and here I am in my eightieth year. I haven't had to do any placer mining yet. And it begins to look like I'm going to get all the way through, as I'd always hoped, without doing any of that damned placer mining. Of course, if I were a physician, particularly an academic physician, I'd have to do the statistics, do the placer mining. But it's amazing what you can do in life without the placer mining if you've got a few good mental tricks and just keep ragging the problems the way Thomas Hunt Morgan did.

我也采用了同样的技巧，现在我已经八十岁了。我还没有进行过任何淘金的工作。而且看来我会一直这么下去，如我一直希望的那样，不会做任何该死的淘金工作。当然，如果我是医生，特别是学术医生，我就不得不做统计，做淘金工作。但如果你有一些好的思维技巧，并像托马斯·亨特·摩根那样不断地解决问题，你会发现生活中不需要淘金工作也能做到很多惊人的事情。

2) Failure to Follow the Fundamental Full-Attribution Ethos of Hard Science 没有遵循硬科学的基本完整归因原则

What's wrong with the way Mankiw does economics is that he grabs from other disciplines without attribution. He doesn't label the grabbed items as physics or biology or psychology or game theory or whatever they really are, fully attributing the concept to the basic knowledge from which it came. If you don't do that, it's like running a business with a sloppy filing system. It reduces your power to be as good as you can be. Now Mankiw is so smart he does pretty well even when his technique is imperfect. He got the largest advance any textbook writer ever got.

曼昆在做经济学时的问题在于他从其他学科中抓取内容却没有归因。他没有将抓取的内容标注为物理学、生物学、心理学或博弈论或其他它们真正来源的学科，完全归因于它们的基本知识。如果你不这样做，这就像经营一个有着混乱文件系统的企业。这会削弱你尽可能变得优秀的能力。现在曼昆很聪明，即使他的技巧不完美，他也做得相当好。他获得了任何教科书作者有史以来最大的预付款。

But, nonetheless, he'd be better if he had absorbed a hard-science ethos, which has been helpful to me.

但是，尽管如此，如果他吸收了对我有帮助的硬科学精神，他会做得更好。

It's like running a business with a sloppy filing system. It reduces your power to be as good as you can be.

这就像经营一个有着混乱文件系统的企业。这会削弱你尽可能变得优秀的能力。

I have names for Mankiw’s approach, grabbing whatever you need without attribution. Sometimes I call it "take what you wish," and sometimes I call it "Kiplingism." And when I call it Kiplingism, I'm reminding you of Kipling's stanza of poetry', which went something like this: "When Homer smote his blooming lyre, he'd heard men sing by land and sea, and what he thought he might require, he went and took, the same as me." Well, that's the way Mankiw does it. He just grabs. This is much better than not grabbing. But it is much worse than grabbing with full attribution and full discipline, using all knowledge plus extreme reductionism where feasible.

我给曼昆的方法起了名字，不加归因地抓取所需的东西。有时我称之为“拿你所愿”，有时我称之为“吉卜林主义”。当我称之为吉卜林主义时，我提醒你们吉卜林的一句诗，大意是这样的：“当荷马弹奏他的花哨琴弦时，他听到人们在陆地和海洋上唱歌，他认为可能需要的东西，他就去拿，就像我一样。”嗯，这就是曼昆的做法。他只是抓取。这比不抓取要好得多。但比起完全归因和完整纪律地抓取，使用所有知识加上在可行时的极端还原论要差得多。

3. Physics Envy 物理学嫉妒

The third weakness that I find in economics is what I call physics envy. And, of course, that term has been borrowed from penis envy as described by one of the world's great idiots, Sigmund Freud. But he was very popular in his time, and the concept got a wide vogue.

第三个我在经济学中发现的弱点是我称之为“物理学嫉妒”。当然，这个术语是从世界上最伟大的白痴之一西格蒙德·弗洛伊德描述的“阴茎嫉妒”借来的。但在他那个时代，他非常受欢迎，这个概念也广为流传。

One of the worst examples of what physics envy did to economics was cause adoption of hard-form efficient market theory. And then, when you logically derived consequences from this wrong theory, you would get conclusions such as it can never be correct for any corporation to buy its own stock. Because the stock price, by definition, is totally efficient, there could never be any advantage. QED. And they taught this theory to some partner at McKinsey when he was at some school of business that had adopted this crazy line of reasoning from economics, and the partner became a paid consultant for the Washington Post. And Washington Post stock was selling at a fifth of what an orangutan could figure was the plain value per share by just counting up the values and dividing. But he so believed what he'd been taught in graduate school that he told the Washington Post it shouldn't buy its own stock. Well, fortunately, they put Warren Buffett on the board, and he convinced them to buy back more than half of the outstanding stock, which enriched the remaining shareholders by much more than a billion dollars. So, there was at least one instance of a place that quickly killed a wrong academic theory.

“物理学嫉妒”对经济学造成的最糟糕的例子之一是采用了硬式有效市场理论。然后，当你从这个错误理论中逻辑推导出结论时，你会得到这样的结论：任何公司购买自己的股票都是错误的。因为股票价格，根据定义，是完全有效的，所以永远不会有任何优势。证毕。他们把这种理论教给麦肯锡的一位合伙人，当时他在某商学院学习，这个商学院采用了这种来自经济学的疯狂推理，该合伙人成为《华盛顿邮报》的付费顾问。《华盛顿邮报》的股票当时以每股价值的五分之一的价格出售，哪怕一个猩猩只需数数价值并除以就能算出。然而，他如此相信他在研究生院学到的东西，告诉《华盛顿邮报》不要买回自己的股票。幸好，他们让沃伦·巴菲特进入董事会，并说服他们回购了超过一半的流通股，这使得剩余的股东们财富增加了超过十亿美元。因此，至少有一个地方迅速终结了错误的学术理论。

And Washington Post stock was selling at a fifth of what an orangutan could figure was the plain value per share by just counting up the values and dividing.

《华盛顿邮报》的股票当时以每股价值的五分之一的价格出售，哪怕一个猩猩只需数数价值并除以就能算出。

It's my view that economics could avoid a lot of this trouble that comes from physics envy. I want economics to pick up the basic ethos of hard science, the full attribution habit, but not the craving for an unattainable precision that comes from physics envy. The sort of precise, reliable formula that includes Boltzmann's constant is not going to happen, by and large, in economics. Economics involves too complex a system. And the craving for that physics-style precision does little but get you in terrible trouble, like the poor fool from McKinsey.

在我看来，经济学可以避免很多这种由于物理学嫉妒引起的问题。我希望经济学能够吸收硬科学的基本精神，完整归因的习惯，而不是物理学嫉妒带来的对无法达到的精确性的渴望。包括玻尔兹曼常数的那种精确、可靠的公式在经济学中大多不会发生。经济学涉及的系统过于复杂。而对物理学式精确性的渴望除了让你陷入巨大的麻烦之外，几乎没有什么用，就像那个麦肯锡的可怜的家伙一样。

I think that economists would be way better off if they paid more attention to Einstein and Sharon Stone. Well, Einstein is easy because Einstein is famous for saying, "Everything should be made as simple as possible, but no more simple." Now, the saying is a tautology, but it's very useful, and some economist—it may have been Herb Stein—had a similar tautological saying that I dearly love: "If a thing can't go on forever, it will eventually stop."

我认为，如果经济学家多关注爱因斯坦和莎朗·斯通，他们会做得更好。爱因斯坦很容易，因为爱因斯坦有句名言：“一切应尽可能简单，但不能过于简单。”这句话虽然是同义反复，但非常有用，一位经济学家——可能是赫伯·斯坦——也有句类似的同义反复的名言，我非常喜欢：“如果一件事情不能永远持续下去，它最终会停止。”

Sharon Stone contributed to the subject because someone once asked her if she was bothered by penis envy. And she said, "Absolutely not. I have more trouble than I can handle with what I've got."

莎朗·斯通对这个话题有所贡献，因为有人曾经问她是否对阴茎嫉妒感到困扰。她回答说：“绝对没有。我已经对我拥有的东西感到很头疼了。”

When I talk about this false precision, this great hope for reliable, precise formulas, I am reminded of Arthur Laffer, who's in my political party, and who takes a mistaken approach, sometimes, when it comes to doing economics. His trouble is his craving for false precision, which is not an adult way of dealing with his subject matter.

当我谈论这种错误的精确性，这种对可靠、精确公式的巨大希望时，我想起了亚瑟·拉弗，他是我的政治党派中的一员，有时在处理经济学问题时采取了错误的方法。他的问题在于他对错误精确性的渴望，这不是一种成人对待其研究对象的方式。

Einstein is easy because Einstein is famous for saying, "Everything should be made as simple as possible, but no more simple.

爱因斯坦很容易，因为爱因斯坦有句名言：“一切应尽可能简单，但不能过于简单。”

The situation of people like Laffer reminds me of a rustic legislator-and this really happened in America. I don't invent these stories. Reality is always more ridiculous than what I'm going to tell you. At any rate, this rustic legislator proposed a new law in his state. He wanted to pass a law rounding pi to an even 3.2 so it would be easier for the school children to make the computations. Well, you can say that this is too ridiculous, and it can't be fair to liken economics professors like Laffer to a rustic legislator like this. I say I'm under criticizing the professors. At least when this rustic legislator rounded pi to an even number, the error was relatively small. But once you try to put a lot of false precision into a complex system like economics, the errors can compound to the point where they're worse than those of the McKinsey partner when he was incompetently advising the Washington Post. So, economics should emulate physics' basic ethos, but its search for precision in physics-like formulas is almost always wrong in economics.

像拉弗这样的人让我想起了一位乡村立法者——这在美国真的发生过。我不编这些故事。现实总是比我将要告诉你的更荒谬。不管怎样，这位乡村立法者提议在他的州通过一项新法律。他想通过一项法律，将圆周率四舍五入到3.2，以便让孩子们更容易计算。你可以说这太荒谬了，把像拉弗这样的经济学教授比作这样的乡村立法者是不公平的。我说，我对这些教授的批评还不够。至少当这位乡村立法者将圆周率四舍五入到一个整数时，错误是相对较小的。但一旦你试图将大量错误的精确性引入像经济学这样复杂的系统中，错误就会累积到比麦肯锡合伙人在无能地建议《华盛顿邮报》时的错误还要严重。所以，经济学应该效仿物理学的基本精神，但在物理学般的公式中寻找精确性在经济学中几乎总是错误的。

4) Too Much Emphasis on Macroeconomics 过于强调宏观经济学

My fourth criticism is that there's too much emphasis on macroeconomics and not enough on microeconomics. I think this is wrong. It's like trying to master medicine without knowing anatomy and chemistry. Also, the discipline of microeconomics is a lot of fun. It helps you correctly understand macroeconomics, and it's a perfect circus to do. In contrast, I don't think macroeconomics people have all that much fun. For one thing, they are often wrong because of extreme complexity in the system they wish to understand.

我的第四个批评是，宏观经济学过于强调，而微观经济学却不够重视。我认为这是错误的。这就像试图掌握医学而不懂解剖学和化学一样。而且，微观经济学的学科很有趣。它帮助你正确理解宏观经济学，而且做起来非常有趣。相反，我认为宏观经济学的人没有那么多乐趣。一方面，他们常常错，因为他们想理解的系统极其复杂。

Let me demonstrate the power of microeconomics by solving two microeconomic problems. One simple and one a little harder. The first problem is this: Berkshire Hathaway just opened a furniture and appliance store in Kansas City, Kansas. At the time Berkshire opened it, the largest selling furniture and appliance store in the world was another Berkshire Hathaway store, selling \$350 million worth of goods per year. The new store in a strange city opened up selling at the rate of more than \$500 million a year. From the day it opened, the 3,200 spaces in the parking lot were full. The women had to wait outside the ladies' restroom because the architects didn't understand biology. It's hugely successful.

让我通过解决两个微观经济学问题来展示微观经济学的力量。一个简单，一个稍微难一些。第一个问题是这样的：伯克希尔·哈撒韦刚在堪萨斯州堪萨斯市开了一家家具和电器店。当伯克希尔开设它时，世界上最大的家具和电器店是另一家伯克希尔·哈撒韦店，每年销售额为3.5亿美元。新店在一个陌生的城市开业时，年销售额超过5亿美元。从开业的那天起，停车场的3,200个停车位就满了。妇女们不得不在女厕外等候，因为建筑师不懂生物学。它非常成功。

Well, I've given you the problem. Now, tell me what explains the runaway success of this new furniture and appliance store that is outselling everything else in the world? Well, let me do it for you. Is this a low-priced store or a high-priced store? It's not going to have a runaway success in a strange city as a high-priced store. That would take time. Number two, if it's moving $500 million worth of furniture through it, it's one hell of a big store, furniture being as bulky as it is. And what does a big store do? It provides a big selection. So what could this possibly be except a low-priced store with a big selection?

好了，我已经给你们出题了。现在，告诉我这家新的家具和电器店为何如此成功，销量超过了全球其他所有店？好吧，让我来告诉你们。它是一家低价店还是高价店？在一个陌生的城市作为一家高价店取得如此成功是不可能的，那需要时间。第二，如果它每年销售价值5亿美元的家具，那一定是个非常大的店，因为家具本来就很占地方。那么一个大店能做什么呢？它能提供大量选择。所以，这显然是一家低价且有大量选择的店。

But, you may wonder, why wasn't it done before, preventing its being done first now? Again, the answer just pops into your head: It costs a fortune to open a store this big. So, nobody's done it before. So, you quickly know the answer. With a few basic concepts, these microeconomic problems that seem hard can be solved much as you put a hot knife through butter. I like such easy ways of thought that are very remunerative. And I suggest that you people should also learn to do microeconomics better.

但是，你可能会问，为什么之前没有人这么做，导致现在才首次实现？答案再次出现在你脑海中：开一家这么大的店需要一大笔资金。因此，之前没有人这么做。因此，你很快就知道答案了。用几个基本概念，这些看似困难的微观经济学问题可以像热刀切黄油一样轻松解决。我喜欢这种既简单又有回报的思维方式。我建议你们也应该更好地学习微观经济学。

Now I'll give you a harder problem. There's a tire store chain in the Northwest that has slowly succeeded over fifty years, the Les Schwab tire store chain. It just ground ahead. It started competing with the stores that were owned by the big tire companies that made all the tires, the Goodyears and so forth. And, of course, the manufacturers favored their own stores. Their "tied stores" had a big cost advantage. Later, Les Schwab rose in competition with the huge price discounters like Costco and Sam's Club and before that Sears, Roebuck and so forth. And yet, here is Schwab now, with hundreds of millions of dollars in sales. And here's Les Schwab in his eighties, with no education, having done the whole thing. How did he do it? I don't see a whole lot of people looking like a light bulb has come on. Well, let's think about it with some microeconomic fluency.

现在我给你们出一个更难的问题。在西北部有一家轮胎店连锁店，经过五十年的慢慢发展取得了成功，那就是莱斯·施瓦布轮胎店连锁店。它一直在稳步前进。它开始与那些由制造所有轮胎的大公司拥有的商店竞争，如固特异等。当然，制造商偏爱自己的商店。它们的“捆绑店”有很大的成本优势。后来，莱斯·施瓦布在与像Costco和Sam's Club这样的大型折扣店竞争中崛起，之前还有西尔斯、罗巴克等。然而，现在的施瓦布有数亿美元的销售额。而莱斯·施瓦布已经八十多岁了，没有受过教育，却做到了这一切。他是怎么做到的？我看没有很多人露出恍然大悟的样子。那么，让我们用一些微观经济学的流畅性来思考一下这个问题。

Is there some wave that Schwab could have caught? The minute you ask the question, the answer pops in. The Japanese had a zero position in tires, and they got big. So this guy must have ridden that wave some in the early times.

施瓦布能赶上某个潮流吗？你一问这个问题，答案就跳出来了。日本在轮胎领域的地位是零，但他们变得很大。所以，这个人一定在早期赶上了这股潮流。

Then, the slow following success has to have some other causes.

然后，慢慢取得成功一定还有其他原因。

[^Les Schwab 莱斯·施瓦布 (1917-2007)]: Leslie Schwab was born in Bend, Oregon. After service in the Air Cadet Corps during World War II, he returned to Oregon and bought OK Rubber Welders, a small tire shop that he turned from a \$32,000-a-year business into one generating $150,000 annually. In the 1950s, Schwab began expanding his business throughout the Pacific Northwest. Through innovations such as profit sharing, "supermarket" product selection, and independence from the tire manufacturing companies, the company now operates over three hundred stores with sales exceeding \$l billion annually. 莱斯利·施瓦布出生在俄勒冈州本德。二战期间在空军军校服役后，他回到俄勒冈州，买下了一家小轮胎店OK Rubber Welders，将其从年营业额32,000美元变成了年营业额150,000美元的生意。1950年代，施瓦布开始在整个太平洋西北地区扩展业务。通过利润分享、“超市”产品选择以及独立于轮胎制造公司的创新，公司现在经营着300多家商店，年销售额超过10亿美元。

And what probably happened here, obviously, is this guy did one hell of a lot of things right. And among the things that he must have done right is he must have harnessed what Mankiw calls the superpower of incentives. He must have a very clever incentive structure driving his people. And a clever personnel selection system, etc. And he must be pretty good at advertising. Which he is. He's an artist. So, he had to get a wave in Japanese tire invasion, the Japanese being as successful as they were. And then a talented fanatic had to get a hell of a lot of things right and keep them right with clever systems. Again, not that hard of an answer. But what else would be a likely cause of the peculiar success?

显然，这个人做了很多正确的事情。他一定利用了曼昆所说的激励的超能力。他一定有一个非常聪明的激励结构来驱动他的员工。还有一个聪明的人事选拔系统等等。他在广告方面一定也很出色。而他确实是。他是个艺术家。所以，他必须赶上日本轮胎入侵的浪潮，日本人非常成功。然后，一个有才华的狂热者必须做对很多事情，并通过聪明的系统保持这些正确的事情。再一次，不是那么难的答案。但还有什么可能是这个独特成功的原因呢？

He must have a very clever incentive structure driving his people. And a clever personnel selection system, etc. And he must be pretty good at advertising. Which he is. He's an artist.

他一定有一个非常聪明的激励结构来驱动他的员工。还有一个聪明的人事选拔系统等等。他在广告方面一定也很出色。而他确实是。他是个艺术家。

We hire business school graduates, and they're no better at these problems than you were. Maybe that's the reason we hire so few of them.

我们雇佣商学院毕业生，他们在这些问题上的表现并不比你们好。也许这就是我们雇佣他们如此少的原因。


Well, how did I solve those problems? Obviously, I was using a simple search engine in my mind to go through checklist-style, and I was using some rough algorithms that work pretty well in a great many complex systems, and those algorithms run something like this: Extreme success is likely to be caused by some combination of the following factors:

那么，我是如何解决这些问题的呢？显然，我使用了一个简单的心智搜索引擎，通过清单式的方式进行思考，并且我使用了一些在许多复杂系统中非常有效的粗略算法，这些算法大致如下：极端成功可能由以下因素的某种组合引起：

* A) Extreme maximization or minimization of one or two variables. Example, Costco or our furniture and appliance store. 极端地最大化或最小化一两个变量。例如，Costco或我们的家具和电器店。

* B) Adding success factors so that a bigger combination drives success, often in nonlinear fashion, as one is reminded by the concept of breakpoint and the concept of critical mass in physics. Often results are not linear. You get a little bit more mass, and you get a lollapalooza result. And, of course, I've been searching for lollapalooza results all my life, so I'm very interested in models chat explain their occurrence.  增加成功因素，使更大的组合推动成功，通常是非线性的，就像物理学中的临界点和临界质量的概念一样。结果往往不是线性的。你获得了一点点更多的质量，你就会得到一个爆发性的结果。当然，我一生都在寻找爆发性的结果，所以我对解释这些结果发生的模型非常感兴趣。
* C) An extreme of good performance over many factors. Example, Toyota or Les Schwab. 在许多因素上的极端良好表现。例如，丰田或Les Schwab。
* D) Catching and riding some sort of big wave. Example, Oracle. By the way, I cited Oracle before I knew that the Oracle CFO (Jeff Henley) was a big part of the proceedings here today. 抓住并乘坐某种大浪。例如，甲骨文。顺便说一句，我在不知道甲骨文的首席财务官（Jeff Henley）是今天会议的重要参与者之前就提到了甲骨文。

Generally I recommend and use in problem solving cut-to-the quick algorithms, and I find you have to use them both forward and backward. Let me give you an example. I irritate my family by giving them little puzzles, and one of the puzzles that I gave my family not very long ago was when I said, "There's an activity in America, with one-on-one contests and a national championship. The same person won the championship on two occasions about sixty-five years apart." "Now," I said, "name the activity." Again, I don't see a lot of light bulbs going on. And in my family, not a lot of light bulbs were flashing. But I have a physicist son who has been trained more in the type of thinking I like. And he immediately got the right answer, and here's the way he reasoned:

通常，我在问题解决中推荐并使用直截了当的算法，我发现你必须正向和反向使用它们。让我给你一个例子。我通过给家人出小谜题来惹恼他们，其中一个我不久前给家人的谜题是：“美国有一种活动，有一对一的比赛和全国冠军赛。同一个人在大约六十五年间两次赢得全国冠军。”然后，我说，“说出这个活动的名称。”再一次，我没有看到很多灵光一现的时刻。在我的家人中，闪光灯也不多。但是我有一个物理学家儿子，他更多地接受了我喜欢的那种思维训练。他立即得到了正确答案，他是这样推理的：

It can't be anything requiring a lot of hand-eye coordination. Nobody eighty-five years of age is going to win a national billiards tournament, much less a national tennis tournament. It just can't be. Then, he figured it couldn't be chess, which this physicist plays very well, because it's too hard. The complexity of the system and the stamina required are too great. But that led into checkers. And he thought, "Ah ha! There's a game where vast experience might guide you to be the best even though you're eighty-five years of age."

这不可能是需要大量手眼协调的任何活动。没有一个八十五岁的人会赢得全国台球比赛，更不用说全国网球比赛了。这是不可能的。然后，他认为这不可能是国际象棋，这位物理学家棋艺高超，因为它太难了。系统的复杂性和所需的耐力太大了。但这引导他想到了跳棋。他想，“啊哈！这是一个经验丰富的人即使八十五岁也能成为最佳的游戏。”

And sure enough, that was the right answer.

果然，这就是正确答案。

Anyway, I recommend that sort of mental puzzle solving to all of you, flipping one's thinking both backward and forward. And I recommend that academic economics get better at very small-scale microeconomics as demonstrated here.

无论如何，我建议你们所有人都进行这种心智谜题解决，正反思考。我建议学术经济学在非常小规模的微观经济学方面变得更好，就像这里展示的那样。

5) Too Little Synthesis in Economics 经济学中的综合性不足

My fifth criticism is there is too little synthesis in economics, not only with matter outside traditional economics, but also within economics. I have posed before two different business school classes the following problem. I say, "You have studied supply and demand curves. You have learned that when you raise the price, ordinarily, the volume you can sell goes down, and when you reduce the price, the volume you can sell goes up. Is that right? That's what you've learned?" They all nod yes. And I say, "Now tell me several instances when, if you want the physical volume to go up, the correct answer is to increase the price." And there's this long and ghastly pause. And finally, in each of the two business schools in which I've tried this, maybe one person in fifty could name one instance. They come up with the idea that, under certain circumstances a higher price acts as a rough indicator of quality and thereby increases sales volumes.

我的第五个批评是经济学中的综合性不足，不仅在传统经济学之外的事物上，而且在经济学内部。我之前在两个不同的商学院班级提出了以下问题。我说，“你们学过供需曲线。你们学到当你提高价格时，通常你能卖出的数量会下降，当你降低价格时，你能卖出的数量会上升。对吗？这就是你们学到的吗？”他们都点头说是。我说，“现在告诉我几个例子，如果你想让销售量上升，正确的答案是提高价格。”然后是一段漫长而可怕的沉默。最后，在我试过的两个商学院班级中，也许五十个人中有一个人能说出一个例子。他们想到了在某些情况下，更高的价格作为质量的粗略指示器，从而增加销售量的想法。

One of the most extreme examples is in the investment management field. Suppose you're the manager of a mutual fund, and you want to sell more. People commonly come to the following answer: You raise the commissions, which. of course. reduces the number of units of real investments delivered to the ultimate buyer, so you're increasing the price per unit of real investment that you're selling the ultimate customer. And you're using that extra commission to bribe the customer's purchasing agent. You're bribing the broker to betray his client and put the client's money into the high-commission product. This has worked to produce at least a trillion dollars of mutual fund sales.

在投资管理领域，最极端的例子之一是这样的：假设你是一家共同基金的经理，你想要卖出更多的基金。人们通常会得出以下结论：你提高佣金，这当然会减少交付给最终购买者的真实投资单位的数量，所以你是在提高你卖给最终客户的每单位真实投资的价格。你在用额外的佣金贿赂客户的采购代理人。你贿赂经纪人背叛他的客户，把客户的钱投入高佣金的产品中。这种方式至少促成了一万亿美元的共同基金销售。

The second interesting problem with synthesis involves two of the most famous examples in economics. Number one is Ricardo's principle of comparative advantage in trade, and the other is Adam Smith's pin factory.

与综合有关的第二个有趣问题涉及经济学中两个最著名的例子。第一个是里卡多的贸易比较优势原则，另一个是亚当·斯密的别针工厂。

This tactic is not an attractive part of human nature, and I want to tell you that I pretty completely avoided it in my life. I don't think it's necessary to spend your life selling what you would never buy. Even though it's legal, I don't think it's a good idea. But you shouldn't accept all my notions because you'll risk becoming unemployable. You shouldn't take my notions unless you're willing to risk being unemployable by all but a few.

这种策略并不是人性中吸引人的一部分，我想告诉你，我在生活中几乎完全避免了它。我认为没有必要把你的生活花在出售你永远不会购买的东西上。即使这是合法的，我也认为这不是个好主意。但你不应该接受我的所有观点，因为这样你可能会面临失业的风险。除非你愿意冒险变得几乎无人雇用，否则不应该接受我的观点。

I think my experience with my simple question is an example of how little synthesis people get, even in advanced academic settings, considering economic questions. Obvious questions, with such obvious answers. Yet, people take four courses in economics, go to business school, have all these I.Q. points, and write all these essays, but they can't synthesize worth a damn. This failure is not because the professors know all this stuff and they're deliberately withholding it from the students. This failure happens because the professors aren't all that good at this kind of synthesis. They were trained in a different way. I can't remember if it was Keynes or Galbraith who said that economics professors are most economical with ideas. They make a few they learned in graduate school last a lifetime.

我认为我对这个简单问题的经验表明，即使在高级学术环境中，人们在考虑经济问题时，也几乎没有综合能力。显而易见的问题，有如此明显的答案。然而，人们修了四门经济学课程，上了商学院，拥有所有这些智商，并写了所有这些文章，但他们在综合能力上却一无是处。这种失败不是因为教授们知道所有这些东西并故意对学生保密。失败的原因是教授们并不擅长这种综合。他们接受的是不同的训练。我记不清是凯恩斯还是加尔布雷斯说过，经济学教授在思想上是最节俭的。他们用他们在研究生院学到的少数几个想法度过了一生。

The second interesting problem with synthesis involves two of the most famous examples in economics. 6) Extreme and Counterproductive Psychological Ignorance

7) Too Little Attention to Second- and Higher-Order Effects 对二阶及更高阶效应关注太少

When I was a meteorologist, I found this stuff very irritating. And economics makes meteorology look like a tea party.

Well, the cost forecast was off by a factor of more than one thousand percent. The cost they projected was less than ten percent of the cost that happened. And the answer is, it wasn't that technology didn't work, it's that the laws of economics caused the benefit from the new looms to go to the people that bought the textiles, not the guy that owned the textile plant. How could anybody not know that if he'd taken freshman economics or been through business school? I think the schools are doing a lousy job. Otherwise, such insanities wouldn't happen so often.

关于这种常见的错误思维，从学术的角度来看，好的一面是商界人士在微观经济学方面更加愚蠢。商业界版本的医保类疯狂情况是，当你拥有一家纺织厂时，有人进来说：“哦，这不是很棒吗？他们发明了一种新的织布机。它会在当前价格下的三年内收回成本，因为它大大提高了纺织品的生产效率。”于是你不断购买这些织布机及其类似产品，长达二十年，你的资本收益率始终保持在百分之四，却从未有所突破。答案是，并不是技术不起作用，而是经济学法则使得新织布机带来的利益转移到了购买纺织品的人手中，而不是拥有纺织厂的人。要是有人上过经济学基础课或者念过商学院，怎么会不知道这一点呢？我认为学校的表现很糟糕，否则这样的疯狂行为不会这么频繁发生。

Usually, I don't use formal projections. I don't let people do them for me because I don't like throwing up on the desk, but I see them made in a very foolish way all the time, and many people believe in them, no matter how foolish they are. It's an effective sales technique in America to put a foolish projection on a desk.

通常，我不使用正式的预测。我不让别人为我做这些预测，因为我不喜欢看到那些荒谬的预测在桌子上，但我常常看到它们以非常愚蠢的方式被做出来，并且许多人相信它们，无论它们多么荒谬。在美国，将一个愚蠢的预测放在桌子上是一种有效的销售技巧。

And if you're an investment banker, it's an arr form. I don't read their projections either. Once Warren and I bought a company, and the seller had a big study done by an investment banker. It was about this thick. We just turned it over as if it were a diseased carcass. He said, "We paid $2 million for that." I said, "We don't use them. Never look at them."

如果你是个投资银行家，这是一种艺术形式。我也不看他们的预测。有一次我和沃伦收购了一家公司，卖家让投资银行家做了一份大规模的研究报告。大约有这么厚。我们只是把它翻过来，就像那是个染病的尸体。他说：“我们为此支付了200万美元。”我说：“我们不用这些东西，从来不看。”

Anyway, as the Medicare example showed, all human systems are gamed, for reasons rooted deeply in psychology, and great skill is displayed in the gaming because game theory has so much potential. That's what's wrong with the workers' comp system in California. Gaming has been raised to an art form. In the course of gaming the system, people learn to be crooked. Is this good for civilization? Is it good for economic performance? Hell, no. The people who design easily gameable systems belong in the lowest circle of hell.

无论如何，正如医保的例子所示，所有的人类系统都被操控，原因深深植根于心理学，在操控中展示了极高的技巧，因为博弈论有着巨大的潜力。这就是加州工伤赔偿制度的问题所在。系统的操控已经上升到了艺术形式。在操控系统的过程中，人们学会了变得狡诈。这对文明有好处吗？对经济表现有好处吗？当然不是。那些设计出容易被操控的系统的人应该下地狱的最低层。

They just don't think about what terrible things they're doing to the civilization because they don't take into account the second-order effects and the third-order effects in lying and cheating.

他们只是不考虑他们对文明造成的可怕后果，因为他们没有考虑到谎言和欺骗的二阶效应和三阶效应。

I've got a friend whose family controls about eight percent of the truck trailer market. He just closed his last factory in California, and he had one in Texas that was even worse. The workers comp cost in his Texas plant got to be double digit percentages of payroll. Well, there's no such profit in making truck trailers. He closed his plant and moved it to Ogden, Utah, where a bunch of believing Mormons are raising big families and don't game the workers' comp system. The workers' comp expense is two percent of payroll.

我有一个朋友，他的家族控制着大约8%的卡车拖车市场。他刚关闭了在加州的最后一家工厂，还有一家在德克萨斯州的工厂，情况更糟。德州工厂的工人补偿成本达到了工资的两位数百分比。而制造卡车拖车并没有那么高的利润。因此，他关闭了工厂并将其搬到犹他州的奥格登，在那里，一群信奉摩门教的人正在抚养大家庭，并且不玩弄工人补偿制度。工人补偿费用只占工资的2%。

Are the Latinos who were peopling his plant in Texas intrinsically dishonest or bad compared to the Mormons? No. It's just the incentive structure that so rewards all this fraud is put in place by these ignorant legislatures, many members of which have been to law school, and they just don't think about what terrible things they're doing to the civilization because they don't take into account the second-order effects and the third-order effects in lying and cheating. So, this happens everywhere, and when economics is full of it, it is just like the rest of life.

那些在德州工厂工作的拉丁裔与摩门教徒相比，本质上是不诚实或坏吗？不是的。这只是激励结构的问题，因为这些无知的立法者制定了奖励这些欺诈行为的制度，很多立法者甚至上过法学院，他们没有考虑到他们对文明的破坏性影响，因为他们没有考虑谎言和欺骗的二阶和三阶效应。所以，这种情况到处发生，当经济学充满了这些问题时，它就像生活中的其他部分一样。

There was a wonderful example of gaming a human system in the career of Victor Niederhoffer in the Economics Department of Harvard. Victor Niederhoffer was the son of a police lieutenant, and he needed to get A's at Harvard. But he didn't want to do any serious work at Harvard because what he really liked doing was, one, playing world-class checkers; two, gambling in high-stakes card games, at which he was very good, all hours of the day and night; three, being the squash champion of the United States, which he was for years; and, four, being about as good a tennis player as a part-time tennis player could be.

在哈佛经济系维克多·尼德霍夫职业生涯中，有一个绝妙的例子说明了操纵人类制度的现象。维克多·尼德霍夫是一个警察中尉的儿子，他需要在哈佛获得A的成绩。但他不想在哈佛做任何严肃的学业，因为他真正喜欢做的是：一、下国际跳棋；二、在高赌注纸牌游戏中赌博，他对这一切都非常在行，不分昼夜；三、成为美国壁球冠军，他已经连续多年获得这一称号；四、作为兼职网球运动员的水平接近专业选手。

This did not leave much time for getting A's at Harvard, so he went into the Economics Department. You'd think he would have chosen French poetry. But remember, this was a guy who could play championship checkers. He thought he was up to outsmarting the Harvard Economics Department. And he was. He noticed that the graduate students did most of the boring work that would otherwise go to the professors, and he noticed that because it was so hard to get to be a graduate student at Harvard, they were all very brilliant and organized and hardworking, as well as much needed by grateful professors.

这使他没有太多时间在哈佛取得A的成绩，所以他进入了经济系。你可能会认为他会选择学习法语诗歌。但请记住，这可是一个可以参加跳棋锦标赛的人。他认为自己能够智胜哈佛经济系。他做到了。他注意到研究生们做了大部分本该由教授完成的乏味工作，而且由于成为哈佛的研究生非常困难，他们都非常聪明、组织有序、勤奋工作，同时也深受感激的教授的青睐。

And, therefore, by custom, and as would be predicted from the psychological force called "reciprocity tendency," in a really advanced graduate course, the professors always gave an A. So Victor Niederhoffer signed up for nothing but the most advanced graduate courses in the Harvard Economics Department, and, of course, he got A, after A, after A, after A, and was hardly ever near a class. And, for a while, some people at Harvard may have thought it had a new prodigy on its hands. That's a ridiculous story, but the scheme will work still. And Niederhoffer is famous: They call his style "Niederhoffering the curriculum."

因此，根据习惯，以及根据被称为“互惠倾向”的心理力量，在真正高级的研究生课程中，教授总是给A。所以维克多·尼德霍夫只报名参加哈佛经济系最先进的研究生课程，当然，他一个接一个地拿到了A，几乎从未上过课。有一段时间，哈佛的一些人可能认为他们有了一个新的神童。这是一个荒谬的故事，但这种计划仍然有效。尼德霍夫因此而闻名：他们称他的风格为“Niederhoffering the curriculum（尼德霍夫式课程）。”

This shows how all human systems are gamed. Another example of not thinking through the consequences of the consequences is the standard reaction in economics to Ricardo's law of comparative advantage giving benefit on both sides of trade. Ricardo came up with a wonderful, non-obvious explanation that was so powerful that people were charmed with it, and they still are because it's a very useful idea. Everybody in economics understands that comparative advantage is a big deal when one considers first-order advantages in trade from the Ricardo effect. But suppose you've got a very talented ethnic group, like the Chinese, and they're very poor and backward, and you're an advanced nation, and you create free trade with China, and it goes on for a long time.

这表明了所有人类系统是如何被操控的。另一个例子是不考虑后果的后果，这体现在经济学中对李嘉图比较优势法则的标准反应上，认为贸易双方都能受益。李嘉图提出了一个精彩而非显而易见的解释，它是如此强大，以至于人们为之着迷，至今仍然如此，因为它是一个非常有用的概念。在考虑李嘉图效应带来的贸易一阶优势时，所有经济学家都明白比较优势的重要性。但假设你有一个非常有天赋的族群，比如中国人，他们非常贫穷落后，而你是一个发达国家，并且你与中国建立了自由贸易，这种情况持续了很长时间。

Now let's follow second- and third-order consequences. You are more prosperous than you would have been if you hadn't traded with China in terms of average wellbeing in the United States, right? Ricardo proved it. But which nation is going to be growing faster in economic terms? It's obviously China. They're absorbing all the modern technology of the world through this great facilitator in free trade, and, like the Asian Tigers have proved, they will get ahead fast. Look at Hong Kong. Look at Taiwan. Look at early Japan. So, you start in a place where you've got a weak nation of backward peasants, a billion and a quarter of them, and, in the end, they're going to be a much bigger, stronger nation than you are, maybe even having more and better atomic bombs. Well, Ricardo did not prove that that's a wonderful outcome for the former leading nation. He didn't try to determine second-order and higher-order effects.

现在我们来看看第二层和第三层的后果。与中国贸易后，你在美国的平均福祉上比以前更繁荣，对吧？李嘉图证明了这一点。但是从经济角度来看，哪个国家的增长速度会更快？显然是中国。他们通过自由贸易这一伟大推动者吸收了世界所有的现代技术，就像亚洲四小龙所证明的那样，他们会迅速取得进步。看看香港，看看台湾，看看早期的日本。所以，你从一个拥有12.5亿落后农民的弱国开始，最终，他们将成为一个比你大得多、强得多的国家，甚至可能拥有更多更好的原子弹。李嘉图并没有证明这对以前的领先国家是一个美妙的结果。他没有尝试去确定第二层和更高层次的影响。

The often overlooked benefits of comparative advantage through free trade were famously revealed by David Ricardo in his 1817 book "The Principles of Political Economy and Taxation":

"In Portugal it is possible to produce both wine and cloth with less work than it takes in England. However, the relative costs of producing those two goods are different in the two countries. In England it is very hard to produce wine, but only moderately difficult to produce cloth. In Portugal both are easy to produce. Therefore, while it is cheaper to produce cloth in Portugal than England, it is cheaper still for Portugal to produce excess wine, and trade it for English cloth. Conversely, England benefits from this trade because its cost for producing cloth has not changed but it can now get wine at closer to the cost of cloth".

通过自由贸易获得的比较优势的好处通常被忽视，然而大卫·李嘉图在他1817年出版的《政治经济学及赋税原理》一书中对此进行了著名的阐述：

“在葡萄牙，生产葡萄酒和布料所需的工作量都比在英格兰少。然而，这两种商品的相对生产成本在两国是不同的。在英格兰，生产葡萄酒非常困难，而生产布料则只是中等难度。在葡萄牙，这两者都很容易生产。因此，尽管在葡萄牙生产布料比在英格兰便宜，但葡萄牙生产过剩葡萄酒并将其换成英格兰的布料更便宜。反之，英格兰也从这项贸易中受益，因为其生产布料的成本没有改变，但现在可以以接近布料成本的价格获得葡萄酒。”

Frequently overlooked is that Ricardo's comparative advantage in "delegating" tasks among nations is equally applicable for managers delegating work. Even if a manager can perform the full range of tasks better himself, it is still mutually advantageous to divide them up.

经常被忽视的是，李嘉图在国家之间“委派”任务的比较优势，同样适用于经理们委派工作。即使一位经理自己可以更好地完成所有任务，将任务分开来做仍然是互利的。

Okay, I'm now down to my eighth objection: too little attention within economics to the simplest and most fundamental principle of algebra. Now, this sounds outrageous, that economics doesn't do algebra, right? Well, I want to try an example-I may be wrong on this. I'm old and I'm iconoclastic-but I throw it out anyway. I say that economics doesn't pay enough attention to the concept of febezzlement. And that I derive from Galbraith's idea. Galbraith's idea was that, if you have an undisclosed embezzlement, it has a wonderful Keynesian stimulating effect on the economy because the guy who's been embezzled thinks he is as rich as he always was and spends accordingly, and the guy that has stolen the money gets all this new purchasing power. I think that's correct analysis on Galbraith's part. The trouble with his notion is that he's described a minor phenomenon. Because when the embezzlement is discovered, as it almost surely will be, the effect will quickly reverse. So the effect quickly cancels out.

好吧，我现在要谈到我的第八个反对意见：经济学对代数中最简单和最基本的原则关注太少。现在，这听起来很离谱，经济学不做代数，对吧？好吧，我想试个例子——我可能在这方面错了。我老了，也有点反传统，但我还是想把它提出来。我说经济学对“伪侵占”（febezzlement）的概念关注不够。这是我从加尔布雷思的想法中得出的。加尔布雷思的观点是，如果你有一个未被揭露的侵占行为，它会对经济产生一种奇妙的凯恩斯式刺激效果，因为被侵占的人认为他像往常一样富有并据此消费，而偷钱的人则获得了新的购买力。我认为加尔布雷思的分析是正确的。他的观点问题在于他描述的是一个次要现象。因为一旦侵占行为被发现（几乎肯定会被发现），效果会迅速逆转。因此，效果很快就会被抵消掉。

But suppose you paid a lot of attention to algebra, which I guess Galbraith didn't, and you think, "Well, the fundamental principle of algebra is, 'If A is equal to B and B is equal to C, then A is equal to C."' You've then got a fundamental principle that demands that you look for functional equivalents, all you can find. So suppose you ask the question, "Is there such a thing in economics as a febezzlement?" By the way, Galbraith invented the word "bezzle" to describe the amount of undisclosed embezzlement, so I invented the word "febezzlement": the functional equivalent of embezzlement.

但是假设你对代数非常关注，而我猜加尔布雷思没有这样做，你会想到，“嗯，代数的基本原则是，‘如果A等于B，B等于C，那么A就等于C。’”那么你就有了一个要求你寻找所有可能的功能等价物的基本原则。所以，假设你问这样一个问题：“在经济学中是否存在所谓的‘伪侵占’？”顺便说一句，加尔布雷思发明了“bezzle”这个词来描述未被披露的侵占金额，因此我发明了“febezzlement”这个词：即侵占的功能等价物。

This happened after I asked the question, "Is there a functional equivalent of embezzlement?" I came up with a lot of wonderful, affirmative answers. Some were in investment management. After all, I'm near investment management. I considered the billions of dollars totally wasted in the course of investing common stock portfolios for American owners. As long as the market keeps going up, the guy who's wasting all this money doesn't feel it because he's looking at these steadily rising values. And to the guy who is getting the money for investment advice, the money looks like well earned income when he's really selling detriment for money, surely the functional equivalent of undisclosed embezzlement. You can see why I don't get invited to many lectures.

在我问“是否存在与侵占功能等价的东西？”这个问题之后，我找到了很多精彩的、肯定的答案。其中一些答案与投资管理有关。毕竟，我很接近投资管理。我考虑了美国投资者在投资普通股票组合过程中浪费的数十亿美元。只要市场持续上涨，那些浪费这些钱的人就不会察觉到，因为他们看到的是这些不断上涨的价值。而对于那些因投资建议而获得资金的人来说，这笔钱看起来像是赚来的收入，实际上是在出售损害换取金钱，这无疑是未被披露的侵占的功能等价物。你可以理解为什么我没有被邀请去做太多演讲。

9. Not enough attention to virtue and vice effects in economics. 在经济学中没有充分关注美德和恶行的影响。

Okay, my ninth objection: not enough attention to virtue and vice effects in economics. It has been plain to me since early life that there are enormous virtue effects in economics and also enormous vice effects. But economists get very uncomfortable when you talk about virtue and vice. It doesn't lend itself to a lot of columns of numbers. But, I would argue that there are big virtue effects in economics. I would say that the spreading of double-entry bookkeeping by the monk, Fra Luca de Pacioli, was a big virtue effect in economics. It made business more controllable, and it made it more honest. Then, the cash register. The cash register did more for human morality than the Congregational Church. It was a really powerful phenomenon to make an economic system work better, just as, in reverse, a system that can be easily defrauded ruins a civilization. A system that's very hard to defraud, like a cash register-based system, helps the economic performance of a civilization by reducing vice, but very few people within economics talk about it in those terms.

好的，这是我的第九个反对意见：在经济学中没有充分关注美德和恶行的影响。从小到大，我一直很清楚，经济学中有巨大的美德效应，也有巨大的恶行效应。但是，当你谈论美德和恶行时，经济学家会感到非常不自在，因为这不是用大量数字能表达清楚的事情。但是，我认为经济学中存在巨大的美德效应。我想说，修士卢卡·帕乔利（Fra Luca de Pacioli）传播的复式记账法就是经济学中的一个重大美德效应。它让商业更加可控，也让它更为诚信。然后是收银机的出现。收银机对人类道德的贡献超过了公理会教堂。它是让经济体系更好运作的一种非常强大的现象，正如反过来说，一个容易被欺诈的系统会毁掉一个文明。一个很难被欺诈的系统，比如基于收银机的系统，通过减少恶行来帮助一个文明的经济表现，但很少有经济学家用这些术语来谈论它。

I'll go further: I say economic systems work better when there's an extreme reliability ethos. And the traditional way to get a reliability ethos, at least in past generations in America, was through religion. The religions instilled guilt. We have a charming Irish Catholic priest in our neighborhood, and he loves to say, "Those old Jews may have invented guilt, but we perfected it."

我进一步说：我认为当经济系统存在一种极端可靠性的伦理时，它的运作会更好。在过去的美国，至少在过去的几代人中，获得这种可靠性伦理的传统方式是通过宗教。宗教植入了内疚感。我们邻居有一个迷人的爱尔兰天主教神父，他喜欢说：“那些古老的犹太人可能发明了内疚感，但我们把它完善了。”

And this guilt, derived from religion, has been a huge driver of a reliability ethos, which has been very helpful to economic outcomes for man.

而这种源于宗教的内疚感，已成为可靠性伦理的重要驱动力，对人类经济成果非常有帮助。

But economists get very uncomfortable when you talk about virtue and vice. It doesn't lend itself to a lot of columns of numbers.

但是，当谈到美德和恶习时，经济学家往往感到不安。这不是一组可以用来填充数字列的数据。

Many bad effects from vice are clear. You've got the crazy booms and crooked promotions-all you have to do is read the paper over the last six months. There's enough vice to make us all choke. And, by the way, everybody's angry about unfair compensation at the top of American corporations, and people should be. We now face various crazy governance nostrums invented by lawyers and professors that won't give us a fix for unfair compensation, yet a good partial solution is obvious: If directors were significant shareholders who got a pay of zero, you'd be amazed what would happen to unfair compensation of corporate executives as we dampened effects from reciprocity tendency.

恶习带来的许多坏影响是显而易见的，比如疯狂的经济繁荣和不正当的推销——你只需阅读过去六个月的报纸就可以知道。有足够的恶习让我们窒息。顺便说一句，所有人都对美国公司高层的不公平薪酬感到愤怒，而且人们应该感到愤怒。我们现在面临各种由律师和教授发明的疯狂治理偏方，这些偏方无法解决不公平的薪酬问题，但一个好的部分解决方案是显而易见的：如果董事会成员是重要股东且不领取报酬，那么当我们减弱互惠倾向的影响时，高管的不公平薪酬问题会发生惊人的变化。

A roughly similar equivalent of this no-pay system has been tried in a strange place. In England, lay magistrates staff the lower criminal courts, which can send you to prison for a year or fine you substantially. You've got three judges sitting up there, and they all get a pay of zero. Their expenses are reimbursed, but not too liberally. And they work about forty half-days a year, as volunteers. It's worked beautifully for about seven hundred years. Able and honest people compete to become magistrates, to perform the duty and get the significance, but no pay.

For one thing, professors-and most of them need money-get appointed directors.

It is not always recognized that, to function best morality should sometimes appear unfair, like most worldly outcomes. Some systems should be made deliberately unfair to individuals because they'll be fairer on average for all of us. Thus, there can be virtue in apparent non-fairness. I frequently cite the example of having your career over, in the Navy, if your ship goes aground, even if it wasn't your fault. I say the lack of justice for the one guy that wasn't at fault is way more than made up by a greater justice for everybody when every captain of a ship always sweats blood to make sure the ship doesn't go aground. Tolerating a little unfairness to some to get a greater fairness for all is a model I recommend to all of you. There are, of course, enormous vice effects in economics. You have these bubbles with so much fraud and folly. The aftermath is frequently very unpleasant, and we've had some of that lately. One of the first big bubbles, of course, was the huge and horrible South Sea Bubble in England. And the aftermath was interesting. Many of you probably don't remember what happened after the South Sea Bubble, which caused an enormous financial contraction and a lot of pain. Except in certain rare cases, they banned publicly traded stock in England for decades. Parliament passed a law that said you can have a partnership with a few partners, but you can't have publicly-traded stock. And, by the way, England continued to grow without publicly-traded stock. The people who are in the business of prospering because there's a lot of stock being traded in casino-like frenzy wouldn't like this example if they studied it enough. ### Talk Nine Revisited

This waggish talk on economics, given in 2003, gave me pleasure as I put it together. But I hope it provided more than harmless fun. I even hope that some shred of my ideas eventually gets into academic economics, not because I want recognition, but because I think academic economics needs some improvement.

这个关于经济学的幽默演讲是在2003年进行的，我在准备这个演讲的过程中感到很愉悦。但我希望它带来的不仅仅是无害的乐趣。我甚至希望我的某些想法最终能够被纳入学术经济学，不是因为我想要获得认可，而是因为我认为学术经济学需要一些改进。

Since the talk was given, I came across a book, published by Alfred A. Knopf in 2005. It was written by a distinguished Harvard economics professor, Benjamin M. Friedman, and dealt with the interplay of economics and morals, much as I wished in my talk. The title of this book is "The Moral Consequences of Economic Growth." As readers will note from the title. Professor Friedman is particularly interested in the impact of economic growth on morals, whereas my interest is mostly in the reverse direction, the impact of morals on economic growth. This difference is not a big deal, because every educated person can see reciprocal effects, for good or ill, between the two factors, creating what is often called either a "virtuous circle" or "vicious circle." Professor Friedman supplies a marvelous quotation on this subject from Rabbi Elazar Ben Azariah: "Where there is no bread, there is no law; where there is no law, there is no bread."

自从那次演讲以来，我读到了一本书，由阿尔弗雷德·A·克诺夫出版社在2005年出版。这本书是由哈佛大学的著名经济学教授本杰明·M·弗里德曼撰写的，内容涉及经济学与道德之间的相互作用，这正是我在演讲中所希望的。书名为《经济增长的道德后果》。从书名可以看出，弗里德曼教授特别关注经济增长对道德的影响，而我更感兴趣的是道德对经济增长的影响。这一差异并不重要，因为每个受过教育的人都可以看到这两个因素之间的互惠效应，不论好坏，这种互惠效应常被称为“良性循环”或“恶性循环”。弗里德曼教授在这个主题上引用了拉比以利亚撒·本·阿扎里亚的一句精彩名言：“没有面包就没有法律；没有法律就没有面包。”

Question: "I have posed at two different business schools the following problem. I say, "You have studied supply and demand curves. [This is the answer I like the most, but never get.] 提高价格并以非法或不道德的方式使用额外收入，通过贿赂采购代理或其他对最终消费者有害的方式来推动销售，即共同基金佣金做法。【这是我最喜欢的答案，但从未得到过。】
